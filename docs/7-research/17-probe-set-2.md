## 1. Slots & Daggers — *what does it do that CloverPit does not?*

**Identity.** Friedemann (solo, dev of SUMMERHOUSE) / **Future Friends Games — CloverPit's publisher** ·
2025-10-24 · **$7.99** · **7,440 all-language, Very Positive (94.2%)**. Corpus n=400 English, funnel 12.

**The answer: it makes runs winnable by decision-making, and it is the only title in this study with
zero steering complaints.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Steering complaints** | **0 of 32 negatives — 0.0%** |
| Funnel ceiling | **99.1%** ("Defeat your first enemy") |
| Zone chain | Plains 91.2% → Cemetery 82.0 → Swamp 74.5 → Desert 68.2 → Sewers 64.6 → Cathedral 62.9 → **Citadel 58.0** |
| **Rarity floor** | **46.8%** — 2nd highest ever measured |
| Median playtime | 5.1h · over 20h **1%** · over 50h 0.5% |

> *(pos, +5, 6.3h)* "Lots of build flexibility and **every run is very winnable with good decision-making**."
> *(pos, +10, 4.3h)* "The game itself is fairly short, however for the price and how fun it is makes it worth it."
> *(neg, +8, 2.4h)* "feels like an early access game. **Fun for exactly 3 hours** then u get through the entire game."
> *(neg, +5)* "appears to be abandonwear… never gotten an update or any new content since its launch."

**Why this matters.** `12` §1 established that the genre's dominant complaint is agency over variance —
40–52% of negatives in every title measured. **Slots & Daggers is the counter-example: same publisher as
CloverPit, same slot-machine core, 0.0%.** The steering complaint is therefore *not inherent to the
genre*. It is a design outcome, and this title avoided it.

**The cost is visible and it is length.** 58% of owners finish the last zone, the rarity floor is 46.8%,
the median is 5.1h and the tail is 0.5%. It bought agency and completion by being short. Feeds **RF-19**.

**Transfer.** STEAL: *"every run is very winnable with good decision-making"* is a design target we can
state and sim-test directly against the G3 band (`10-economy-rework.md` §F) — it is **RF-4** phrased as a
player would phrase it.

## 2. Dungeon Clawler — *does the top of the price band work?*

**Identity.** Stray Fawn Studio / Stray Fawn Publishing · 2026-04-30 · **$14.99** ·
**3,931 all-language, Very Positive (89.1%)**. Corpus n=400 English, funnel 39, rarity floor 1.4%.

**The answer: yes — the most expensive title in the probe set has by far the best retention.**

| | Dungeon Clawler | probe-set peers |
|---|---|---|
| Median playtime | **11.2h** | Slots & Daggers 5.1h · Tharsis 6.5h · Bills 4.5h |
| **Over 20h** | **37%** | next best Sol Cesto 28% |
| **Over 50h** | **13.8%** | 2nd best in the whole study, behind only Balatro's 33.5% |
| Rarity floor | 1.4% | deep completion space |
| Funnel | per-character wins: Scrappy 52.1% → Sir Bunalot 41.7 → Clawcula 40.1 → Felina 29.9 → Dolly 22.0 | |

> *(pos, +15, 1.3h)* "Great game. **Good price as well.** There is actually much more strategy involved with the updates they've added. Don't listen to people saying the whole game is luck based, because it isn't."
> *(neg, +5, 5.5h)* "**Was really fun up until you beat it for the first time then every run basically felt the exact same**."

**Two readings.** First, `07-business-and-roadmap.md`'s $8–13 band has a working example just above it —

observed failure in the genre.

**Transfer.** STEAL: per-character win achievements give a clean public read on which characters actually
get played — worth mirroring if SBR ever ships multiple starting archetypes.

## 3. Sol Cesto — *the steering question in another game's words*

**Identity.** Tambouille / Goblinz Publishing · 2026-04-10 · **$13.99** ·
**4,957 all-language, Very Positive (91.1%)**. Corpus n=400 English, funnel 21, rarity floor 3.6%.

**The answer: it removes the steering complaint by making the distribution visible at the moment of
choice.** Store copy: *"choose one of four rows to move into. You'll then land randomly on one of the four
tiles in that row… It's up to you to weigh the risks of each tile."* You see the four possible outcomes,
you pick the row, the draw is random.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Steering complaints** | **1 of 41 negatives — 2.4%** |
| `luck_vs_skill` overall | 23.5% (discussed constantly, resented rarely) |
| Median playtime | 11.0h · over 20h 28% · over 50h 2.0% |
| `depth_thin` / `boredom_quit` | 20.8% / 6.0% — the highest boredom rate in the probe set |

> *(pos, +14, 9.2h)* "**Genuinely best art direction of any game i've played in awhile** highly recommend for that alone. Game gets a little bit monotonous mid game, not enough variety run to run."

**This is the same mechanism as Buckshot Roulette's known-counts/unknown-order (`05` §1, STEAL S4), and it
is the third independent instance.** See §6.

Second observation, unprompted: Sol Cesto's most-upvoted positive leads with **art direction**, exactly as
Nubby's Number Factory's does (`16` §2). Two beloved titles in this study are praised first for how they
look, not what they do.

## 4. Tharsis — *what aged badly?*

**2,511 all-language, Mostly Positive (70.1%) — the lowest-rated title in the entire study.**

**The answer: nothing aged. It was built on dread without agency, and that reads as unfairness.**

|---|---|---|

| Positive rate | **70.1%** | **last** |
| "Contact Light — Reach Mars" (the win) | **28.8%** | — |

> *(pos, +6, 5.4h)* "**The dice are evil. Tharsis feels downright unfair at times.** Still there's a solid fundamental survival game here."
> *(pos, +9, 67.9h)* "I love this game, I hate this game. I love to hate it, I hate to love it."

**Set it against Buckshot Roulette.** Both are built on a high-dread random resolution. Buckshot gives the

evidence this lane has produced about SBR's signature moment.

Also: Tharsis's 28.8% win rate is the closest any reference comes to SBR's ruled 5–8% band, and it is the
worst-received title in the study. Feeds **RF-4**.

## 6. Synthesis — three new proposals

### RF-17 — The fix for the steering complaint is legibility at the decision point, not more control

**Three independent titles, three different genres, one mechanism.**

| Title | Mechanism | Steering complaints |
|---|---|---|
| Buckshot Roulette | live/blank **counts announced**, order hidden | — (`cash_out`/`near_miss` dead here; qualitative in `05` §1) |
| Sol Cesto | choose a row; **all four possible tiles visible** before the draw | **2.4%** of negatives |
| Slots & Daggers | *"every run is very winnable with good decision-making"* | **0.0%** of negatives |
| — versus — | | |
| Luck be a Landlord | symbol pool opaque | **44%** of negatives |
| Dungeons & Degenerate Gamblers | deck outcomes opaque | **52%** of negatives |
| Tharsis | dice | 8.3% of negatives, and 38.8% luck language overall |

**What the research argues.** `12` **RF-12** asked whether to give the player bounded steering of the item
stream. This is a better and cheaper answer: **players do not resent randomness they can price.** They
resent randomness they cannot see coming. Every low-complaint title makes the distribution explicit at the
moment of choice; every high-complaint title hides it.

**SBR may already be protected — by construction.** `02-betting-math.md`'s four-number model puts true
probability, offered odds, stake and payout on the table, and `00-vision` pillar 3 requires every mechanic
be mathematically legible. **The risk is not the maths; it is whether the surface shows it at the moment
of the decision.** That makes this a UI requirement on `surething-design.md` and `tv-design.md`, not an
economy change.

**Ruling requested.** Adopt as a design law: *at every point where the player commits capital, the
distribution they are committing against is visible on the same screen.* Cheaper than RF-12's item-stream
steering, and it may make RF-12 unnecessary. **Falsifier:** if SBR's surfaces already do this everywhere,
the law is free and closes; if they do not, the gap list is the work.

### RF-18 — Dread without agency reads as unfairness, and it is worth about 22 points

**Canon.** `00-vision` pillar 1 — the sweat is sacred, resolution is not skippable by default.
`04-the-sweat.md` — the outcome is sampled first; a "mid-sweat agency ladder" is **PROPOSED, 2026-07-07,
still unratified**.

**Evidence.** Buckshot Roulette and Tharsis are both high-dread random resolutions. Buckshot puts a
decision inside the tension: **92.2% positive**, and **48.8% of owners voluntarily took a 50/50 on
themselves**. Tharsis gives dice and no decision inside the roll: **70.1% positive**, the study's highest
luck-complaint rate (38.8%), highest too-hard rate (13.0%) — and the study's highest dread language (6.0%).
**Tharsis is not failing to produce tension. It produces the most tension in the study and is liked least.**

**What the research argues.** SBR's sweat is a resolution phase the player watches. The cash-out is
currently the *only* agency inside it, and `04`'s agency ladder is still unratified after thirteen months.
**On this evidence, the agency ladder is not a nice-to-have — it is the difference between Buckshot and
Tharsis.** This binds tightly to **RF-7**: the cash-out must sometimes be wrong to take, or the one piece
of agency inside the sweat is a formality.

**Ruling requested.** Ratify a mid-sweat agency ladder for v1 scope, or state explicitly that the cash-out
is the whole ladder and accept RF-7 as its load-bearing constraint.

### RF-19 — Price is punished for mismatching depth, not for being high or low

**This resolves the tension between RF-11 and RF-15 rather than adding to it.** Both poles work:

| Shape | Titles | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Cheap and honestly short | Bills Must Be Paid $6.99/4.5h · Buckshot $2.99/4.0h · Slots & Daggers $7.99/5.1h · Scritchy $6.99/11.7h · Nubby $4.99/7.4h | 92–97% positive |
| Expensive and genuinely deep | **Dungeon Clawler $14.99 / 11.2h median / 37% over 20h / 13.8% over 50h** | 89.1% positive |
| **Expensive and shallow** | **Gambonanza $14.99 / 3.3h median / 34.4% under 2h** | **75.1% — worst in the study** |

**What the research argues.** There is no genre-wide "right price." There is a **relationship** — and the
only titles punished are those whose price outruns their depth. `07-business-and-roadmap.md`'s $8–13 band
is defensible as a *decision* but its "genre-proven" framing is not supported in either direction.

**Ruling requested.** Replace the band with a rule Allen can actually hold a design to: **pick the depth
first, then price to it** — and decide which of the two shapes above SBR is. That decision is the same one
as **RF-15** and it gates **RF-3** (what the store page says), so all three should be ruled together, as
`14` already argued.
