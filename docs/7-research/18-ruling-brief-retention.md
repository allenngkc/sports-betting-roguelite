# 18 — Ruling brief: where retention comes from

**Lane:** research · **Date:** 2026-08-13 · **Status:** RULING REQUESTED — one decision, one word
**Bundles:** RF-5 · RF-8 · RF-12 · RF-14 (previously four separate proposals)
**Sources:** `06` §3, `12` §1–2 §4, `14` §3, `15`, `17` §6 · **Does not change:** any design doc — proposals only

---

## The question

**Canon protects the sweat and the information axis; the research says neither of those is what
brings a player back tomorrow — so does SBR name and fund a retention layer, and how much of it in v1?**

---

## The evidence, one line each

| | What it says | The number that carries it |
|---|---|---|
| **RF-8** | Tension is not retention. | Buckshot Roulette is the sweat, isolated and executed superbly: highest tension language in the study (2.2%), **lowest compulsion language (2.6%, one sixth of the other three)**, median lifetime **4.0h**. |
| **RF-14** | Comprehension is not retention either. | Insider Trading is SBR's information axis with the sport swapped for a stock: **highest luck-complaint rate in the study (30.5%)**, highest onboarding friction (11.4%), and **8% of the people who beat it come back to beat it again — Balatro's figure is 61%**. |
| **RF-5** | *Having* a ladder tracks retention; depth does not. | No ladder: Gambonanza 3.3h, Buckshot 4.0h, D&DG 7.0h, RACCOIN 7.6h. With one: CloverPit 9.1h, LbaL 10.4h, **Balatro 25.1h**. But LbaL's twenty rungs reach the same endpoint as Balatro's short steep ladder (12.4% vs 12.1%) and retain **2.4× less** — so rung count is not the variable. Strong form withdrawn in `12` §2. |
| **RF-12** | The item economy is the other retentive system, and its known failure mode is the genre's #1 complaint. | Agency-over-variance is the top negative theme in **every** title measured: **52%** of D&DG's negatives, **44%** of LbaL's, 40% of Gambonanza's. The recurring sentence is not "too hard" — it is *"there's no way I can see to increase your odds of getting specific symbols."* |

**Why they are one decision.** The three retentive references all produce compulsion (15–19% addiction
language, 7.6–25.1h medians) from **item economies and ladders that never touch their resolution phase**.
RF-8 and RF-14 remove the two systems canon currently protects from the retention job. That leaves exactly
the two systems RF-5 and RF-12 are about. Rule the four together or the answer is incoherent.

**Where canon currently stands on those two.** The ladder is *"ascension-style difficulty tiers **post-v1**"*
(`01-core-loop.md`). The item stream is a **dealt-hand shop** — items are offered, not chosen (`design/11`) —
under the bounded-p doctrine (`10-economy-rework.md` §E). Both retentive systems are, today, outside v1 or
outside the player's reach. Neither omission looks deliberate; they read as side effects.

**One sharp adjacency.** SBR's ruled 5–8% win band sits about where the references put their *optional top
rung* (Balatro Gold Stake 12.1%, CloverPit Ascension 9.1%) — with nothing below it. A ladder gives the base
game somewhere lower to sit. (Full ruling on the band is RF-4, not this brief.)

## The options

| | Ruling | What it commits to | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Adopt** | Name the retention layer (item economy + ladder + collection) as a protected budget alongside pillar 1 — **and** pull both systems into v1: 2–3 ladder rungs with the base game below the ruled band, plus one bounded steering mechanism on the item stream. | Highest. Economy scope + gate re-run + rung UI. |
| **B** | **Adopt-narrow** *(recommended)* | Name the layer. Pull **the ladder only** into v1. Hold the steering question for RF-17, which argues the cheaper fix is legibility at the decision point, not more control. | Config + a gate campaign re-run + whatever surface names the rungs. No economy change. |
| **C** | **Name-only** | Add the clause to pillar 1 so the omission is on the record. No v1 scope change; both systems stay post-v1. | Nothing. |
| **D** | **Reject** | Pillar 1 stands as written. The sweat and the information axis are the bet, and this lane's inference from other games' corpora does not override it. | Nothing. |

---

## Recommendation: **B**

Three reasons.

**The ladder is the part the evidence actually supports.** Every title with a ladder outlasts every title
without one, and that survives the correction that killed RF-5's strong form. Depth doesn't matter, which is
what makes it cheap — two or three rungs buys the effect, and `12` §2 suggests front-loading the first one.

**The steering half is about to get a cheaper answer.** RF-17 (next in the queue) finds three titles across
three genres with near-zero steering complaints, and the shared mechanism is not more control — it is that the
distribution is **visible at the moment of the commitment**. SBR's four-number model may already satisfy that
by construction, which would make RF-12 a UI gap list rather than an economy change. Spending economy scope
now, before that check, is the expensive order to do it in.

**Naming costs nothing and is the load-bearing half.** Pillar 1 is not wrong — *"all juice budget flows here
first"* is the right priority for making the moment great. It just provides no budget for the systems that
make a player come back, and on this evidence those are strictly different systems. Nothing gets cut by
saying so.

**What B risks.** If RF-17's legibility law turns out to be already-satisfied *and* insufficient, the steering
complaint comes back and RF-12 has to be ruled anyway, later, when the economy is harder to move. **C** is the
honest choice if v1 scope is genuinely closed — it keeps the record straight and costs nothing. **A** is right
only if you already believe the dealt-hand shop is a problem and want it fixed regardless of RF-17.

**Falsifier on the whole bundle.** RF-14 rests on one title (n=387) that reviewers describe as under-baked
and awaiting patches. If a patched Insider Trading moves its numbers, the "comprehension ≠ retention" half
weakens. Cheap to re-check; I would re-pull in three months.
