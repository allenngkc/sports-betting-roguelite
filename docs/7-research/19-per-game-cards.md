# 19 — The fifteen titles, in the per-game format

**Lane:** research · **Date:** 2026-08-13 · **Status:** REFERENCE — reformat of work already committed
**Format (Allen, 2026-08-13):** per game — *addicting elements · fun core · quoted mechanic · our
application*, with honest gaps stated. **Nothing here is new research.** Every figure is carried from
`02`–`05`, `08`–`11`, `15`–`17` and is dated 2026-08-12. No new proposals are raised; where a card lands on
a decision, it points at the existing RF.

**Why the format earns its keep.** Splitting *addicting* from *fun* forces the two apart on every card, and
across fifteen titles they separate cleanly rather than travelling together — which is the lane's central
finding (RF-8, RF-14) stated fifteen times instead of argued once. See §4.

**Reading rule, carried from `13` §5.** † marks an **all-language** store rating; unmarked sentiment is a
**recent-English corpus**. Never compare across the two — mixing them is the error that produced `14`'s
Insider Trading correction.

## 2. The cards

### 1 — Balatro · 2024-02-20 · $11.99 · 196,738 Overwhelmingly Positive

**Addicting elements.** Permanent in-run growth first (Jokers, hand levels, vouchers), then the eight-stake
ladder, then collection. `addiction` language **18.8% — highest in the study**. Median lifetime 25.1h;
**33.5% over 50 hours**, the largest tail measured anywhere.

**Fun core.** Building a machine that breaks the game's own arithmetic — and then choosing how hard the next
one should be. The base game is generous (90.0% reach Ante 4, 74.7% reach Ante 8, **71.7% win**); the
difficulty is a menu the player orders from.

**Quoted mechanic.** The stake ladder — measured, not quoted: 71.7 → 43.7 → 30.2 → **12.1%**, roughly halving
each rung. What the corpus actually says out loud is about the build:
> "Its just a funny little card game. I wont get addicted right?" — 365.8h

**Our application.** The ladder is the retention engine canon defers post-v1 — **RF-5**, now bundled in `18`.
Ante-8 → win converts at **96%**: the last round is a coronation, not a wall (**RF-9**). Base-most-players-beat
plus an opt-in ladder is the shape the G3 band should take.

**Gaps.** Exact resolution timings UNREACHED — Allen has played this one and `07` q1 closes it in a minute.
The per-ante base-chip table did not extract cleanly and is recorded UNREACHED. Near-miss lexicon null
(instrument failure, not absence). Note also that **no verbatim in our 1,000-review sample names the ladder** —
the mechanic that retains is not the mechanic players narrate.

### 2 — CloverPit · 2025-09-26 · $5.99 · 25,496 Very Positive

**Addicting elements.** The deadline clock above all — **56.0% of owners reached the Death Countdown**. Then
the learnable pity schedule, then the 666 counterattack (90.0% have taken one; 48.5% lost a million to it).
`addiction` 15.1%; `gambling_real` **32.4%, highest in the study**.

**Fun core.** The pressure is not in the machine, it is in the room. The slot is where you act; the tension is
authored by a persistent, visible, always-worsening obligation between spins. `design/09` recorded the player
phrase for the mid-game turn: *"no longer gambling — engineering fate."*

**Quoted mechanic.** A fixed, public requirement curve that announces linear play is dead —
75 → 200 → 666 → 2,222 → 12,500 → 33,333 → 66,666 → 200,000 → 1,000,000 (avg ×3.3, identical every run).
The cost of the players who never decode the pity schedule:
> *(negative)* "It's fun but the percentages are just a smokescreen." — 1.8h

**Our application.** SBR already owns both halves — a room and a debt — and canon currently treats the sweat
as its only tension source. The room and phone surfaces are the second one. Pillar 4 gets a number here:
leaning into the gambling theme costs **~7.5 points of positive sentiment** against Balatro and buys a far
louder cultural conversation (1M+ copies in under two months).

**Gaps.** Timings UNREACHED (Allen has played it). Its ascension layer is a **single rung at 9.1%** — CloverPit
is the ceiling case for a thin meta, not a model for one. The 30.9% escape rate is still 4–6× SBR's ruled band.

### 3 — RACCOIN: Coin Pusher Roguelike · 2026-03-31 · $9.59 · 4,495 Very Positive

**Addicting elements.** Big-number spectacle (28.2% have scored a million on one coin), a deep collection
almost nobody finishes (0.6–0.7%), a second-chance ticket economy, and unlock drip. `addiction` 17.1% — but
`boredom_quit` **3.3%, highest in the set** and a >50h tail of **3.5%, the thinnest of the compulsive titles**.

**Fun core.** Cascade: one commit resolving into many small results arriving over a second or two. And the fun
core is *contested by its own audience* — the second most-upvoted negative (+89) says the big scores feel
underwhelming, which is evidence against this lane's own reading and is recorded rather than dropped.

**Quoted mechanic.** Tickets exchanged for coins to avoid failing a round — a second-chance economy on a
meta-currency.
> "number go up, big number good, big number fun, much play, much enjoyment" — 17.6h

**Our application.** **COMPS already exists** and is the natural vehicle for the second-chance economy.
The cascade grammar matches legs arriving one at a time in `04-the-sweat.md`. And 15 rounds pairs with the
highest boredom language and the thinnest tail in the set: `01-core-loop.md`'s open round-count question
should not default upward.

**Gaps.** No play by anyone on this project — confidence MEDIUM. The 81.6% recent figure straddles a
**publicised telemetry incident** (the single most-upvoted negative, +130) and is a trust story, not a design
one; all-time sentiment is Very Positive. Title age (4.5 months) is not separable from the retention-tail
comparison — that ranking is **unproven**. Pity levers UNREACHED.

### 4 — Buckshot Roulette · 2024-04-04 · $2.99 · 123,710 Overwhelmingly Positive

**Addicting elements.** Almost none, and that is the finding. `addiction` **2.6% — one sixth of the other
three Tier-1 titles**. What exists is sunk-cost chasing, which the developer names in the UI: 39.3% of owners
opted into the achievement literally called **"Chasing Losses"**, and 11.2% hold **"Overdose"**.

**Fun core.** The decision inside the tension. **48.8% of all owners deliberately shot themselves at 50/50** —
because the rule makes the tense play the correct play: a self-shot on a blank retains the turn, so when
blanks outnumber lives, maximum drama and maximum EV are the same action.

**Quoted mechanic.** Verbatim from the funnel: **"Coin Flip — successfully shoot yourself with a blank at
50/50 odds," 48.8%.** The nearest optional-flourish achievements in the same funnel run 5.7–9.6%.

**Our application.** **RF-7** stated exactly: if the cash-out is priced at or above the fair value of the
remaining legs, cashing out is always correct and the sweat becomes a formality the optimal player skips.
Also: known counts / unknown order is our four-number model's own shape, and naming the compulsion lever
honestly in the UI is free satire.

**Gaps.** No play; confidence MEDIUM. This is also the cautionary half — 4.0h median, **0.4% over 50 hours**,
no meta layer at all. It is what SBR looks like if pillar 1 wins and the economy layer stays thin. Repeated
photosensitivity complaints are a defect to avoid, not a technique to copy.

### 5 — Gambonanza · 2026-05-01 · $14.99 · 1,601 Mostly Positive

**Standing correction:** this is a **chess roguelike, not a gambling one**. This lane proposed it from its
name; **no finding here may be used as the gambling genre's failure case** (`08` §1.5).

**Addicting elements.** Weak. Median **3.3h**, **34.4% under two hours**, only 4.2% over twenty, and 75.1%
positive — the softest reception in the study.

**Fun core.** Hard to locate from outside, and that is itself the result: the funnel shows real depth
(0.1% rarest of 53; ROOK 10.6%, KNIGHT 6.4%) that **is not legible in the first three hours**, which is all
most reviewers played. 60.1% of reviews invoke Balatro — praise included ("Chess Balatro" is the compliment).

**Quoted mechanic.** None earned. The verbatim that carries is commercial, and it is the corpus's
most-upvoted review:
> *(positive, +64)* "Fun game, but **don't buy it here. You can get it on the app store/play store for $6** and it comes with a copy for PC."

**Our application.** Three warnings, no steals. A cheaper copy on another storefront caps your Steam price
(`07-business-and-roadmap.md` plans mobile as a v2 wave — **sequencing matters**). The audience sets the
comparison frame and ours is already set. And a pacing complaint can arrive **24 minutes in** — **RF-10**,
against a pillar that makes resolution deliberately slow.

**Gaps.** n=706, the full recent-English pool but small — every percentage here has a wider error bar than
the Tier-1 figures. Nothing about the gambling genre, per the correction above.

### 6 — Luck be a Landlord · 2023-01-06 · $9.99 · 11,517 Very Positive

**Addicting elements.** `addiction` 16.8%, `gambling_real` 24.2% (2nd only to CloverPit), median 10.4h with a
10.6% >50h tail. 186 achievements — the largest funnel in the study — and a twenty-floor ladder.

**Fun core.** Assembling a symbol engine that beats the rent. The satire needs no exposition; the top
positives are political jokes.

**Quoted mechanic.** Rent due every N spins, escalating, miss it and the run ends — the direct ancestor of
SBR's per-settle debt payments. Its most-upvoted negative is the whole finding:
> *(negative, +14, 3.4h)* "**Symbols are too random, it's too hard to try and plan a build because there's no way I can see to increase your odds of getting specific symbols** so it just gets frustrating"

**Our application.** After three years of live tuning the **curve stops being the failure point entirely** —
`too_hard` runs **1.5%, the lowest in the study** — and the failure migrates to steering, which is 44% of all
negatives. `design/11`'s dealt-hand shop is exactly the structure that complaint is about: **RF-12**. If SBR
ships rungs, front-load the first (LbaL's biggest drop is floor 1→2 at −10.0 points, decelerating to −0.7 by
floor 20).

**Gaps.** This probe **falsified this lane's own RF-5 strong form**: twenty rungs, 10.4h, against Balatro's
short ladder and 25.1h. Rung count is not the variable. "After tuning" is inferred from the title's age and
live-service history, not from a changelog anyone read.

### 7 — Dungeons & Degenerate Gamblers · 2024-08-08 · $7.49 · 3,500 Very Positive

**Addicting elements.** Modest — median 7.0h, 7.2% over fifty. The drip is the named-opponent chain rather
than an item ladder.

**Fun core.** Real blackjack that visibly cheats, and a player who cheats back. `blackjack` language runs
37.5% — players discuss the vocabulary constantly and complain about it in only 15 of 161 negatives.

**Quoted mechanic.** Three mechanisms, all verbatim-backed. The opponent chain (Manager **88.5%** → Bouncer
58.3 → … → win a run 32.2): the tutorial is a person you beat. The achievement **"Clumsy — Get a blackjack
then hit and bust," held by 40.9%**: the classic beginner error is rewarded. And the licence:
> *(positive, +105, 5.1h)* "the best way to describe it is thats its like the yugioh anime where **everyone is basically making up the rules as they go**."

**Our application.** Pillar 2's tutorial answer, buildable from a cast SBR already has — gurus and bookies as
a named chain, an achievement for the classic beginner error (a −EV favourite parlay; cashing out a ticket
that then wins), and a crooked book whose rules visibly improvise.

**Gaps.** The caveat is larger than the finding: **blackjack needs no tutorial because the culture is the
tutorial**, and arbitrage/+EV/hedging/getting-limited have no such prior. This is evidence the *mechanism*
works, not that the problem is the same size — **RF-13**. Public data cannot tell us whether players learned
blackjack or already knew it, and that is unresolvable from outside.

### 8 — Scritchy Scratchy · 2026-03-18 · $6.99 · 14,629 Very Positive

**Addicting elements.** The highest measured anywhere: `addiction` **19.5%, above Balatro's 18.8%**, with the
**lowest refund-window share in the study (2.5%)** and a median of 11.7h. Also `gambling_real` 26.0%.

**Fun core.** Maximum juice with near-zero decision density. It works — and it runs out. Nobody complains
early: the depth complaints arrive at 14.6h, 8.6h and 10.4h played.

**Quoted mechanic.** **"Don't read the fine print" — taking a loan, held by 81.4% of owners.** The ceiling, in
its own audience's words:
> *(negative, 14.6h)* "Wish it was a little bit longer, more content… **Prestige is lazy game design**"
> *(positive, +51, 15.4h)* "Game so fun it made me realize I have a gambling addiction, 10/10 definitely would recommend"

**Our application.** The **$8–13 band's premise** is what this challenges, not its number — **RF-11**. A $6.99
title tops the study on compulsion language and refund share and took 14,629 reviews in five months. Steal
the repeated-death comedy beats and the honest naming of the debt's first use.

**Gaps.** Its **rarity floor is 12.1%** against 0.1–1.5% everywhere else — essentially nothing in the game is
unreachable, and that caps the **tail** (3.6% over 50h), not the median. And this probe cannot see revenue,
which is the half of the price question that actually decides `07-business-and-roadmap.md`.

### 9 — Insider Trading · 2026-02-18 · $12.89 · 632 all-language Very Positive (80.4%)†

**Addicting elements.** Weak exactly where SBR's canon predicts strength. `addiction` **6.7% — 9th of the
eleven titles then measured** — against `luck_vs_skill` **30.5%** (`15` ranks this 1st of eleven; across the
full fifteen it is **2nd**, behind Tharsis's 38.8%) and `onboard` friction **11.4%, 1st across the fifteen**.
Median 3.5h; 29% under two hours.

**Fun core.** Not being right — blowing yourself up legibly. Both top positives are self-inflicted
catastrophes the player caused and understood; none celebrates a correct read of the market.

**Quoted mechanic.** From the developer's own copy: *"only you can decide when to cash out and secure your
profits"*, and an Aftermarket draft *"often taking upside alongside risk. Some cards strengthen your deck.
Others weaken it."* From the corpus:
> *(positive, +33, 4.0h)* "**I rug-pulled myself.** Turned my balance into -200k."

**Our application.** This is the nearest shipped implementation of SBR's stated soul, in our price band, with
six difficulty rungs, twelve characters, 120+ cards and a 0.1% rarity floor — **and it returns 8% of the
people who beat it, against Balatro's 61%.** That is **RF-14**: name the information axis's compulsion
partner. Steal the downside-bearing draft offer for the dealt-hand shop, and Greed as one player-controlled
dial that raises gain and risk together (it pairs with RF-7).

**Gaps.** n=387, the smallest corpus in the study. Multiple reviewers are explicitly waiting on patches, so
this reads as **under-baked as much as mis-designed** — the falsifier is a re-pull in three months and costs
one request. The store rating is all-language; this lane already published one correction for reading the
English-only slice as the band.

### 10 — Nubby's Number Factory · 2025-03-07 · $4.99 · 19,198 all-language Overwhelmingly Positive (97.4%)†

**Addicting elements.** Conspicuously absent. `addiction` **7.2% — less than half** Balatro's 18.8%,
CloverPit's 15.1% or Scritchy Scratchy's 19.5% — on a title with the second-best sentiment in the study.
**Beloved without being described as compulsive.** `gambling_real` 0.4%, the lowest measured, on a game Steam's
own community tagged *Gambling*.

**Fun core.** Art, sound and feel — the corpus praises those first — wrapped around big-number spectacle and a
joke about the sun exploding.

**Quoted mechanic.** The pitch, verbatim from store copy: *"make numbers, so you can make bigger numbers, so
you can make even BIGGER numbers."* Five balls per round against a production quota; miss it and the sun
explodes. And the failure mode of a **watched physics resolution**:
> *(negative, +7, 10.3h)* "**a recent update to the physics basically made the game RNG** in deciding whether or not youll get past the first 20 rounds."

**Our application.** Two, and they are the most encouraging pair the lane has. First, this is **the business
comp that matches `00-vision`'s actual constraint** — solo, self-published, $4.99, and it cleared our 500-review
target roughly thirty-eight times over (**RF-16**). Second, `04-the-sweat.md`'s outcome-first integrity rule —
the engine samples first, the drama only narrates — **structurally immunises SBR against the complaint that
sank a chunk of Nubby's negatives**, and it should be stated in `04` as a defence rather than as an
architecture note.

**Gaps.** **The funnel cannot measure this title's completion rate** — there is no "beat the game once"
achievement, only "beat it on every supervisor" (2.5%). Recorded UNREACHED, not estimated. The "five balls per
round" figure comes from a player review, not developer copy — lower-grade evidence. The recent filter means
the negative themes are partly a snapshot of one physics patch.

### 11 — Slots & Daggers · 2025-10-24 · $7.99 · 7,440 all-language Very Positive (94.2%)†

**Addicting elements.** Thin, and deliberately so: median 5.1h, **1% over 20 hours**, 0.5% over fifty, and a
**rarity floor of 46.8%** — the second-highest ever measured. Nearly nothing is out of reach.

**Fun core.** Winnable by decision-making. It is **the only title in the entire study with zero steering
complaints — 0 of 32 negatives.**

**Quoted mechanic.** The design target, in a player's words:
> *(positive, +5, 6.3h)* "Lots of build flexibility and **every run is very winnable with good decision-making**."

**Our application.** Same publisher as CloverPit, same slot-machine core, 0.0% steering complaints against the
genre's 40–52%: **the dominant complaint is a design outcome, not a genre tax.** That quoted sentence is
**RF-4** phrased as a player would phrase it, and it is directly sim-testable against the G3 band.

**Gaps.** It bought agency and completion **by being short** — *"Fun for exactly 3 hours then u get through
the entire game"* — and a second negative calls it abandonware. n=400, 12-row funnel: a thin instrument
compared with the Tier-1 titles.

### 12 — Dungeon Clawler · 2026-04-30 · $14.99 · 3,931 all-language Very Positive (89.1%)†

**Addicting elements.** The best retention in the widened probe set and second-best in the whole study:
median **11.2h**, **37% over 20 hours**, **13.8% over fifty** — behind only Balatro's 33.5%. Rarity floor 1.4%,
so the completion space is real.

**Fun core.** A claw machine as the draw mechanic, run per character (Scrappy 52.1% → Dolly 22.0%).

**Quoted mechanic.** The price defence and the failure, both from the corpus:
> *(positive, +15, 1.3h)* "Great game. **Good price as well.** …Don't listen to people saying the whole game is luck based, because it isn't."
> *(negative, +5, 5.5h)* "**Was really fun up until you beat it for the first time then every run basically felt the exact same**."

**Our application.** The **top of the $8–13 band has a working example just above it**, so RF-11's revenue half
is unsettled against the ceiling as well as the floor. And that negative is the **winner-return problem in a
player's own words** — the same failure `15` measured numerically at 8% in Insider Trading. It is now the most
consistently observed failure in the genre. Per-character win achievements are worth mirroring if SBR ever
ships multiple starting archetypes.

**Gaps.** n=400. The winner-return read here is one quote plus one numerically-measured title — it is a
pattern with two points, not a rate measured in this corpus.

### 13 — Sol Cesto · 2026-04-10 · $13.99 · 4,957 all-language Very Positive (91.1%)†

**Addicting elements.** Median 11.0h with 28% over 20 hours — but `boredom_quit` **6.0%, the highest in the
probe set**, and `depth_thin` 20.8%. It holds people, then tires them.

**Fun core.** Pricing visible risk. `luck_vs_skill` runs 23.5% overall — discussed constantly — while steering
appears in only **1 of 41 negatives (2.4%)**. Players talk about luck without resenting it.

**Quoted mechanic.** Store copy, verbatim: *"choose one of four rows to move into. You'll then land randomly
on one of the four tiles in that row… **It's up to you to weigh the risks of each tile**."* You see the four
possible outcomes, you pick the row, the draw is random.

**Our application.** The third independent instance of one mechanism — with Buckshot's known-counts/unknown-order
and Slots & Daggers — and it is **RF-17**: at every point where the player commits capital, the distribution
they are committing against is visible on the same screen. `02-betting-math.md`'s four-number model may already
satisfy this; the risk is the surface, not the maths, which makes it a UI requirement on `surething-design.md`
and `tv-design.md` rather than an economy change.

**Gaps.** n=400. Unpriced observation: this is the **second** beloved title in the study whose top positive
leads with art direction rather than mechanics (the other is Nubby). The lane has no instrument for that and
has not tried to build one.

### 14 — Tharsis · 2016-01-11 · $14.99 · 2,511 all-language Mostly Positive (70.1%)†

**Addicting elements.** None worth the name — the lowest-rated title in the study, with `luck_vs_skill`
**38.8%, `too_hard` 13.0% and `depth_thin` 24.0% — all three the highest ever measured.**

**Fun core.** Dread, genuinely produced: `dread_tension` **6.0%, the highest ever measured — above Buckshot
Roulette's 2.2%**. **Tharsis is not failing to produce tension. It produces the most tension in the study and
is liked least.**

**Quoted mechanic.** Dice, with no decision inside the roll. From a **positive** review:
> *(positive, +6, 5.4h)* "**The dice are evil. Tharsis feels downright unfair at times.** Still there's a solid fundamental survival game here."

**Our application.** **RF-18**, and it is the most direct evidence this lane has about SBR's signature moment.
Set against Buckshot: same high-dread random resolution, and the only variable is agency inside it — worth
**~22 points of sentiment** (92.2% vs 70.1%). `04-the-sweat.md`'s mid-sweat agency ladder has been PROPOSED and
unratified since 2026-07-07; on this evidence it is the difference between the two titles, not a nicety.
Separately: Tharsis's 28.8% win rate is the **closest any reference comes to SBR's ruled band** (4.5–8%
since 2026-08-08), and it is the worst-received title in the study (feeds **RF-4**).

**Gaps.** A 2016 title read through a 2026 recent-English corpus (n=400): a decade of genre drift sits between
it and the rest of the cohort. "Nothing aged" is an inference from the complaint mix, not a longitudinal
measurement — no before/after exists in this data.

## 3. Gaps that belong to the set, not to any one card

- **No play by this lane, on any title.** Allen has played Balatro and CloverPit; `07` remains the only
  first-hand channel and is unanswered.
- **The instruments are not equal across the fifteen.** Tier-1 and Tier-2 used n=1,000 recent English; the
  widened probes used n=400–600; Insider Trading n=387. Playtime medians and lexicon percentages from the
  small corpora carry visibly wider error bars and should not be ranked against the large ones as if equal.
- **Two lexicon families are known-dead and are never reported:** `near_miss` (1 hit in 4,000) and `cash_out`
  (12 in 4,000). A null from either is instrument failure, not absence.
- **Resolution timings are UNREACHED everywhere.** Fifteen for fifteen. Two are answerable by Allen in a
  minute; the rest need a footage pass nobody has run.
- **Revenue is unmeasured everywhere.** Review counts and sentiment are not net income, and the price
  questions (RF-11, RF-15, RF-19) all turn on the half this lane cannot see.

## 4. What the reformat made visible

**No new proposals.** Three observations, each routed to an RF already open.

**The split holds on all fifteen.** Sorting *addicting* from *fun* separates the set rather than tracking it:
the three titles with the lowest compulsion language — Buckshot 2.6%, Insider Trading 6.7%, Nubby 7.2% — are
liked at 92.2%, 80.4% and 97.4%, and every high-compulsion title is carrying an item economy, a ladder, or
both. Stated once it is an argument; stated fifteen times it is a shape. → **the `18` bundle (RF-5/8/12/14)**.

**Three of the fifteen have the same fun core, and it is not the one canon names.** Insider Trading's top
