## 1. Method note — what is measured here and what is not

The predecessor lane's closing ledger names its own dominant defect: *numbers quoted past their
evidence*, six named instances. This plan is written against that.

- Every probability, price and row count below is **computed from the shipped model** —
  `MatchModel.EnumerateScores` / `RawPoisson` / `TrueProbability`, replicated exactly, swept over
  the full latent box the generator can produce (`p ∈ [0.25, 0.75]`, goal tempo `[0.85, 1.15]`,
  corner tempo `[0.80, 1.20]`, discipline `[0.80, 1.20]`). Ranges are **worst case over that box**,
  not means — because `Offer()` throws on a single bad seed, not on a bad average.
- **What this instrument cannot see (C25):** it is exact arithmetic over the pricing model, so it
  says nothing about *bot selection*, *exposure*, *win rates*, or *wall time*. Every claim in §7
  about the campaign is arithmetic on op counts, explicitly labelled as such, and is to be
  **measured before it is believed**. This lane has missed campaign wall-clock three times running;
  no wall-time prediction appears in this document.
- One correction already owed, made here rather than shipped: an intermediate read of mine called
  the correct-score tail "roughly 4× longer than anything the shipped board carries." That compared
  against the two-way tail (8.8×) and ignored the anytime-scorer market, which already reaches
  **62.6×**. The correct figure is **1.5×** at a 1% floor, and *inside* the existing tail at 2%.

## 2. The five frozen markets — verdicts

| Market | Verdict | Why, in one line |
|---|---|---|
| **Handicap** | **UNFREEZE, ±1.5 only** | ±0.5 is arithmetically identical to the moneyline; ±2.5 and beyond **crash** `Offer()` at reachable seeds. |
| **Team totals** | **UNFREEZE** | Prices cleanly everywhere; brings clean sheets and "fails to score" into the vocabulary for free. |
| **Double chance** | **STAYS FROZEN** | Not a judgement call — it is arithmetically dead under no-draws. Two duplicates and a crash. |
| **Correct score** | **UNFREEZE if R3 allows** | Prices well and lands exactly in the item ecology's band; the draw-free grid is the visible cost. |
| **HT/FT** | **STAYS FROZEN** | The only candidate needing new sampler draws — it alone would force a golden-seed re-pin. |

### 2.1 Handicap — unfreeze at ±1.5, and only ±1.5

Because a draw is unrepresentable, the goal margin is never 0. Three consequences, all measured:

| Line | True-p range (worst case over the latent box) | Offered odds | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| ±0.5 | 0.2500–0.7500 | 1.27–3.81 | **Identical to the moneyline.** `P(margin > 0.5) = P(home wins) = p`, exactly. A duplicate row. |
| ±1.5 | favourite 0.5757–0.9144 / dog 0.0856–0.4243 | 1.04–1.65 / 2.24–11.13 | **The only genuinely new pair.** Ships. |
| ±2.5 | favourite 0.8041–**0.9778** | **0.97**–1.18 | **CRASHES.** `p ≥ 0.95238` makes odds `≤ 1.0` and `MatchModel.Offer` throws by construction. |
| ±3.5 | favourite 0.9249–**0.9954** | **0.96**–1.03 | Crashes harder. |

The ±2.5 crash is not a tail risk. It needs `p` near 0.75 with goal tempo near 0.85 — both
ordinary draws from the generator — and the campaign locks on the order of 10⁷ matchups. It would
fail in minutes, every time.

**Ship: home −1.5 / away +1.5 and away −1.5 / home +1.5. Two pairs, four offers.**

### 2.2 Team totals — unfreeze

All lines price legally across the whole latent box.

| Line | Over p | Under p | Under odds | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.5287–0.8915 | 0.1085–0.4713 | 2.02–8.78 | Under 0.5 **is** the clean sheet / "fails to score" market. |
| 1.5 | 0.2048–0.6706 | 0.3294–0.7952 | 1.20–2.89 | Clean. |
| 2.5 | 0.0613–0.3843 | 0.6157–**0.9387** | **1.01**–1.55 | Legal, but the under pays 1% at the worst seed — a junk row. |

**Ship 0.5 and 1.5 per team; drop 2.5.** Recommend the clean sheet is *named* as team-total under
0.5 rather than shipped twice under two names — one predicate, one row.

### 2.3 Double chance — stays frozen, and the reason is arithmetic

Under no-draws:

- **1X** = `P(home win ∨ draw)` = `P(home win)` = **the moneyline home price, exactly** (0.2500–0.7500).
- **X2** = **the moneyline away price, exactly**.
- **12** = `P(home ∨ away)` = **1.0000**, at every seed → odds **0.95** → `Offer()` throws.

There is no version of this market that is not either a duplicate row or a crash. It does not need
a design ruling; it needs draws, which is R2.

### 2.4 Correct score — the market that makes the fiction visible

It prices well. At a 2% floor the board carries **10–15 rows**, all in the longshot band that the
item ecology is built around (Longshot Larry's Photo triggers at odds ≥ 3.0), and the modal score
prices at **4.35–8.65**.

The cost is what the grid looks like. Both columns below are the same latents (`p = 0.50`, tempo
1.0); the left is what we would ship, the right is what the model's own goal rates imply before the
winner is conditioned on:

| Rank | Shipped (no draws) | What the latents imply |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **1-0 · 12.2%** | **1-1 · 12.2%** |
| 2 | 0-1 · 12.2% | 0-1 · 9.1% |
| 3 | 2-1 · 11.1% | 1-0 · 9.1% |
| 4 | 1-2 · 11.1% | 1-2 · 8.3% |
| 5 | 2-0 · 8.3% | 2-1 · 8.3% |
| 6 | 0-2 · 8.3% | **0-0 · 6.7%** |

**No 0-0. No 1-1. No 2-2.** On a totals or handicap row the missing draw is invisible — it is
absorbed into a number. A correct-score grid is the one surface that *enumerates the outcome
space*, so it displays the hole directly. This is why R3 is a fiction call: the math is sound, and
the question is whether a book in this world can show a score grid with no 1-1 on it.

**If R3 says no, correct score stays frozen and the rest of this plan is unaffected.**

### 2.5 HT/FT — stays frozen, and it is the only one that would cost a re-pin

Every other market in this plan grades as a predicate over the **existing** `MatchStatLine`.
HT/FT does not: there is no half-time state anywhere in the engine. Adding one means new draws in
`SampleStatLine`, which breaks its documented fixed six-draw contract and re-pins the golden seeds
in *both* `engine.tests/GoldenSeedTests.cs` and the Unity `GoldenReplay` fixture.

Freezing it is what keeps this lane's re-baseline to **bot behaviour only**. That is the single
biggest scope decision in this plan and it is the reason the rest of it is cheap.

## 4. R2 — the no-draws constraint should stand

**Recommendation: stands. Not because it is cheap to keep, but because lifting it is a different lane.**

What it costs, measured: the latents themselves imply a draw mass of **22.6%–28.4%, mean 25.6%** —
which is what real football does (~24–26%). The model is not wrong about football. It computes a
quarter of the outcome space and then refuses to render it, redistributing that mass onto decisive
scores. That is why 1-0 sits where 1-1 belongs in §2.4.

What lifting it would take — an inventory, named (C18), not a count:

1. `MatchStatLine` — the constructor **throws** on `homeGoals == awayGoals`, and `Winner` is a
   two-valued `Side`. Both become three-valued.
2. `Matchup.Result` (`Side?`) and every consumer that reads a winner as a side.
3. `MatchModel.EnumerateScores` — drops the `h > a` truncation; the winner draw in `SampleStatLine`
   becomes a three-way categorical. **The six-draw contract changes → golden re-pin, engine and Unity.**
4. `OddsMath.OverroundOdds` — two-way by signature.
5. `SkilledStrategy.EstimateProbability` — the sharp's *only* estimator is two-way de-vig
   (`impl / (impl + implOpp)`). A three-way moneyline has no opposite; the estimator is rewritten,
   and every gate banded against that bot's win rate re-reads.
6. G3 (4.5–8% band), G6 (skilled +2pp) and G5's exemplar all sit on that surface.

That is a lane. It is not a step inside a vocabulary slice, and it cannot share this lane's single
campaign. **Named consequence of keeping it:** correct score ships draw-free, or does not ship.

## 6. What the gates demand of every new market

**G7 is the binding constraint.** Every shipped `MarketKind` must be either exercised by the skilled
bot (`LegsPlaced > 0`) or on the named bot-excluded list *with a reason* — no silent skips.

Under exact de-vig every selection ties at −vig EV, so a non-moneyline market is only ever chosen
once an owned item has pulled the sharp off the moneyline — in practice Longshot Larry's Photo,
which triggers at **odds ≥ 3.0**. This splits the new vocabulary cleanly:

- **Reachable** (prices clear 3.0 at real seeds): handicap dog side, team total under 0.5, correct
  score, margin 2 and 3+, team corners, player 2+ goals. These should earn G7 coverage through the
  same arm-B reservoir tie-break that corners and cards now use.
- **Will read zero** (never clears the band): odd/even favourite side, team total over 0.5, margin 1.
  These need the **BTTS/M1 treatment** — a *measured* exclusion, where the random bot's leg count
  proves the market is **declined, not blocked**. An asserted exclusion is exactly the silent skip
  G7 exists to catch.
- **One-way markets** (correct score, margin, player 2+) inherit the AnytimeScorer exclusion shape
  and S24's single-column surface ruling.

**Two implementation traps that would kill the campaign, not just skew it:**

1. `SkilledStrategy.Opposite()` **throws** on any kind not in its switch
   (`sim/SkilledStrategy.cs:583-591`). Every new kind must be added there or explicitly `continue`d
   like AnytimeScorer. A miss is a crash mid-campaign, not a bad number.
2. `RandomStrategy.RandomBotSelection` picks uniformly over all non-scorer offers. Adding one-way
   markets silently changes what "random" means and re-weights the exclusion evidence in §6. The
   one-way markets must be excluded there too, or the exclusion evidence measures the wrong thing.

## 7. The offer lookup is O(n²) — BUILT AND MEASURED, and the measurement demotes this section

> **UPDATE 2026-08-12, after Phase 0 ran. Read this before the arithmetic below.**
>
> The fix is built (`engine/Domain.cs` — one lazily-built board index, replacing both linear
> scans *and* the re-enumeration behind `Leg.TrueProb`). It is **behaviour-neutral, proven not
> assumed**: a full 7-gate campaign at n=400 produces a report **bit-identical** to the
> pre-fix reference — every gate, every figure — with only the timestamp and wall-time lines
> differing. Reproduced three times.
>
> **It bought no measurable speed, and I cannot claim it did.** Wall times for identical work on
> the fixed tree: **40.27 / 46.60 / 52.54 s — a 30% spread**, replicating the 27–28% this machine
> showed at n=4,600 and n=10,000. The pre-fix run sat at 40.69 s, i.e. *inside* that spread. An
> instrument with 30% resolution cannot see a 1% effect (C32).
>
> **So this section overstated its risk, and the correction is mine.** The 5.1× below is correct
> arithmetic about the wrong quantity: it counts operations in the candidate loop, and the
> measurement says that loop is not where the campaign spends its time. Removing *both* lookups
> entirely moved the total by less than the noise floor — which bounds their combined share of
> campaign cost at well under the 30% this instrument can resolve.
>
> **What stands:** the fix stays, because it is free, behaviour-neutral, and strictly better code
> — `Leg.TrueProb` was re-running a full score enumeration on every access while the answer sat
> stored on the `MarketOffer` the whole time. It is **insurance for the wider board, not a
> speedup**, and it must not be reported as one.
>
> **What is still unmeasured:** the cost at ~90 offers. No board that size exists yet, so no
> instrument here can read it. Phase 3's campaign is the first honest measurement, and the
> prediction to score against it is written down now: **the expanded board costs less than 30%
> extra wall time**, i.e. the change stays inside this machine's noise floor.

### The original arithmetic, kept because it is what the fix was built on

`Matchup.Odds(selection)` is a **linear scan** over `Markets` (`engine/Domain.cs:275-280`). The
skilled bot calls it twice per candidate — once for the selection, once for its opposite — inside a
loop over every offer. Cost is quadratic in board size.

| | offers | bot ops per matchup-round |
|---|---|---|
| Today | 36 | ~1,600 |
| After this slice | ~90 | ~8,100 |
| | | **~5.1×** |

That is arithmetic on op counts, **not** a wall-time prediction — this lane has missed campaign
wall-clock three times and will not do it a fourth. The measured campaign cost today is 20.2 and
25.6 minutes at the ruled floor of 10,000, with a 27–28% spread on identical work.

Two fixes, both cheap, both before any market lands:

1. The candidate loop already holds `offer.Odds`. One of the two scans is free to delete.
2. The opposite-side lookup wants a dictionary on `Matchup`, built once with the board.

**Phase 0 exists to do this and to re-measure the campaign before the vocabulary changes it**, so
the re-baseline reads market changes rather than a performance regression wearing their clothes.

## 8. Domain shape — the one structural change

`MarketSelection` is `{ Kind, Line, Choice, PlayerIndex }`. Team totals and handicap need a **team**;
correct score needs a **score**. Neither fits.

Recommended: extend the struct with **named** fields — `Team` (`Side?`), `ScoreHome`, `ScoreAway`
(ints, −1 when unused) — rather than packing meaning into `Line` or overloading `PlayerIndex`.
S22 struck the packed display string on exactly this principle; a packed *numeric* field is the
same defect in another costume.

Blast radius, named: `Equals` / `GetHashCode` / `==` (mechanical), the `MarketProbEst` dictionary
key in `sim/BotState`, `Opposite()`, `MatchModel.Fields()`, `DisplayLabel()`. All mechanical, all
compile-enforced.

## 12.1 Feasibility — yes, and three things make it cheaper than "three-way rewrite" sounds

**a. The sampler's six-draw contract survives.** `SampleStatLine` spends one `NextDouble()` on
`< TrueHomeProb` today; a three-way class pick spends **one `NextDouble()` too**, compared against
two cumulative bounds instead of one. Corners and cards untouched. **Six draws, before and after.**
Golden seeds re-pin once because the *values* move; nothing shifts position in the stream. Same
bounded, intended re-pin F_0.4.0 Phase 1 performed.

**b. `Side` does not become three-valued.** It is a *team* designator — rosters, per-team corners
and cards, `PlayerSide`, `TeamStats`. What becomes three-valued is the match **result**.

| | `Side.Home/Away` | `.Winner` | `.Result` | `MarketChoice.Home/Away` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| production (engine, sim, console, Unity runtime) | 38 | 3 | 19 | 22 |
| tests (engine.tests, Unity tests) | 186 | — | — | — |

A new `MatchResult { Home, Draw, Away }` and roughly **40 production sites**. The 186 test
references are mechanical.

**c. The draw probability is free and already correct.** No new dial, no invented number: the
latents already imply `P(h == a)` = **22.6%–28.4%**, already varying the right way (higher for even
matches), already matching real football (~24–26%). The engine computes that mass today and refuses
to render it.

**`p` keeps its exact current meaning.** `pH = p·(1−pD)`, `pA = (1−p)·(1−pD)`, `pDraw = pD`. Since
every match today is decisive, `p` *already* means "P(home | decisive)". The `[0.25, 0.75]`
generator band and everything tuned against it survive untouched.

**d. Today's moneyline is not deleted — it becomes DRAW NO BET.** Measured: the draw-no-bet
favourite prices at **p 0.250–0.750**, which is today's moneyline exactly. So the 1X2 market is
*added* beside a market that keeps its current price surface, and **the sharp keeps a two-way
instrument to price** while the three-way one arrives. That materially lowers the risk in §12.5.

**e. The bot's estimator generalizes rather than gets rewritten.** Its concept is already
"normalize the implied probs of the offered set"; only the set size is wrong. `Opposite(selection)`
becomes `Siblings(selection)` — the full mutually-exclusive, exhaustive outcome set. **One change
serves draws, double chance, and every future multi-way market.** Correct score stays bot-excluded:
its board is truncated by the 2% floor, so it is *not* exhaustive and normalizing it would
over-normalize.

## 12.2 The model change, precisely

1. `MatchResult { Home, Draw, Away }`; `MatchStatLine.Winner` returns it; the constructor's
   `"Soccer stat lines cannot draw in v1"` throw is **deleted** — it is the constraint, in code.
2. `MatchModel.EnumerateScores` gains the `h == a` class beside `h > a` and `a < h`.
3. `SampleStatLine` picks the class three-way from its existing single draw.
4. `Matchup.Result` becomes `MatchResult?` — null still means "not locked", `Draw` now means drawn.
5. `MarketChoice` gains `Draw`; moneyline becomes 1X2, and draw-no-bet ships as its own kind using
   the existing `Leg.IsVoided` machinery.
6. `OddsMath.OverroundOdds` gains an n-way form; the two-way one stays for two-way markets.

## 12.4 What reprices — and what does not

Every goal-derived market moves. **Corners and cards do not** (score-independent), so half the
board is stable across the re-baseline.

| | today | with draws |
|---|---|---|
| Total goals OVER 2.5 | 49.2–67.8% | 40.3–60.3% |
| Total goals UNDER 2.5 | 32.2–50.8% | **39.7–59.7%** |
| BTTS yes | 37.7–56.2% | **44.8–62.1%** |

Largest single shift: total goals under 2.5, **50.5% → 59.4%**.

**The economy move — this is the bill, not the code.** A 0.65 favourite moneyline leg goes
**0.650 → 0.485** (price **1.47 → 1.96**, 1.34× longer). The sharp's 3-leg favourites parlay — the
plan that funds the payment schedule — goes **0.275 → 0.114 win probability, 2.41× rarer.**

EV per leg is **unchanged at −vig by construction**. What moves is **hit rate and variance**, which
is exactly what G3 (skilled run win 4.5–8%) and G6 (martyr ≤ skilled +2pp) are banded on.

**Which way G3 moves is not predictable from here and will not be predicted.** Longer prices and a
lower hit rate push run win rate in opposite directions, and the sharp simultaneously gains double
chance and draw-no-bet as survival instruments he does not have today.

## 12.5 The batching answer — the doctrine holds, but draws needs its own re-baseline

**The doctrine is about markets, and it is preserved in full: the vocabulary still lands as ONE
batch in ONE campaign.** No market dribbles in; no repeated restarts.

**Draws is not a market addition.** It is the model underneath the markets, and it moves the
measuring stick every gate is calibrated against. That is why it needs its own re-baseline, and
saying so is the answer to Allen's question.

**They cannot honestly share one campaign.** If a gate goes red on a combined landing, nothing in
the report distinguishes "draws did it" from "the vocabulary did it" — the exact failure the
predecessor lane's closing ledger is about: instruments that cannot tell you what they measured.
Batching these two would build one.

**Structure — two re-baselines, only one of them a market campaign:**

- **Re-baseline A — DRAWS.** Engine model change, bot estimator generalized, goldens re-pinned.
  **Campaign A** at the ruled floor. Exit: 7/7 gates with 7 verdicts on a draws economy. If a band
  moves, that is Allen's re-rule or a payment-curve re-tune (`--grid`) — and it is the expensive
  branch.
- **Re-baseline B — THE VOCABULARY.** All markets at once, on the settled economy. **Campaign B**
  at the ruled floor. Exit: 7/7 with verdicts, plus G7 coverage or a *measured* exclusion for every
  new kind.

| Scenario | Campaigns | What decides it |
|---|---|---|
| Gates hold inside their bands | **2** | best case |
| G3 or G6 lands out of band | **4–6** | payment-curve re-tune or a band re-rule by Allen |

Precedent for the pessimistic row: the G3/G5/G6 family took campaigns at 1,000 / 4,600 / 10,000 /
18,500 plus re-runs to settle, and no two of the three were fixed by the same move.

**No wall-clock estimate.** Measured times only, per the standing order. For scale, not prediction:
one campaign at the ruled floor measured 20.2 and 25.6 minutes; this machine spreads 27–30% on
identical work.

   set, and normalizing it would silently over-normalize. This is a *measured* exclusion in G7's
   sense, not an asserted one.
