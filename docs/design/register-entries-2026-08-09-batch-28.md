## S71-am2 — §6's "exactly once" counts SPEAKERS, not occurrences. No violation

**Observation A resolved: there is nothing to fix.**

Four first-person strings exist: `MY MARKS`, `MY BETS`, `MY BETS · READ-ONLY TV MIRROR`, and
`ROUND LOCKED — WATCH MY BETS`. §6 as written says first person appears *exactly once and it is not the
product* — read literally, that is four violations.

**Read for its purpose, it is zero.** The clause protects one thing: **that there is exactly one
first-person speaker on the surface, and that it is the player, never the product.** `MY MARKS` is his
column. `MY BETS` is his tickets, and the other two are that destination's name in use. **One voice,
four appearances, and the voice is his.** A second first person would be the product claiming an *I* —
`MY RECOMMENDATION`, `MY PICK` — and none exists.

**Ruled: no violation. Nothing is renamed.** `MY BETS` is a destination's proper name and a screen's
name is not the product speaking.

**§6's clause is amended in `surething-design.md` to say so**, because as written it invites this false
positive — and it produced one today, from a lane doing careful work and reporting honestly rather than
acting.

## C40 — A rule states the property it protects; a proxy is labelled as one

**Law** · DD 2026-08-09. Two founding cases, **both this seat's own phrasing**, both inside three days.

| rule as written | what it protects | what the phrasing does |
|---|---|---|
| T70: *"no term repeated across the pair"* | the state line carries information the requirement does not | flags `1/2 TEAMS SCORED`, which is correct |
| §6: *"first person exactly once"* | one first-person speaker, and it is the player | flags `MY BETS`, which is correct |

**Both are detectable proxies standing in for the property.** Both generalise wrongly the moment they
leave the case that produced them, and **both were faithfully applied by leads** — which is the cost.
A lane reading a proxy builds a gate on the proxy: T70's would have failed a correct count line, §6's
would have failed a screen's own name.

**Ruled: where a rule at this seat names a detectable test alongside the property, the test is labelled
a proxy, and the property governs.** Batch 24 already did this once by instinct — the second-person
pronoun guard was granted *"recorded so it is not mistaken for the ruling: this is a proxy."* That
instinct becomes the rule.

**This binds rule-writing at this seat, not work at the lanes.** Its benefit to a lead is the opposite
of a burden: **a labelled proxy tells them exactly what to gate and what not to escalate.**
