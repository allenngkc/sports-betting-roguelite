## 1 — THE REVEAL. Does the theater show the MATCH, or only the ticket's slice?

**What we measured.** Same seed, both arms finished `REGULATORS 5 — SPREADSHEETS 1`. The goals
ticket saw the 30' goal. **The corners ticket never saw it** — its scoreline read `0 — 0` for **86%
of the watch**, then stepped `0—1` → `5—1` in two beats at the whistle. Not a bug: the revealed
ledger discloses what the ticket rides on, and a corners ticket does not ride on goals.

**Why this is the biggest call in the phase — it is also the fix for the flat watch.** The corners
arm has **no resting state**: one beat classification across fourteen consecutive windows. The goals
arm departs from calm and returns to it, and that contour is what a watch is made of. **A goal the
corners player does not need is exactly the departure his watch is missing.** One change answers
both findings.

- **A — the scoreline is always true; everything else stays ticket-keyed.** *(recommend)* The score
  is never withheld. Stats rows, player detail and flavour keep following the ticket. Removes the
  falsehood, removes the two-step result at the death, and supplies the resting state.
- **B — show the whole match.** Every event reveals on the true clock. Cleanest principle, largest
  change, and the most competing detail for a player watching one narrow bet.
- **C — no change.** Every non-goal ticket keeps watching a nothing-match.

**Cost of A, stated plainly:** the ledger's rule stops being *"reveal what the ticket rides on"* and
becomes *"the score is always true, the rest follows the ticket."* That is a rule changing shape,
not a tweak.

## 2 — EMPTY GROUPS. Print the markets the house is not offering today?

Every real book silently drops them. Our law says the slate must not be misrepresented — and **nine
of fifteen market kinds currently have no home at all**, measured on frames.

- **Yes — print them, with counts.** *(recommend)* `CORNERS ….. 11` where there are offers,
  `CORNERS ….. no prices offered` where there are none. Costs about six lines, and BTTS already has
  5.8 rows of slack to hold them.
- Counts only; empty groups vanish as they do everywhere else. The player cannot tell *not offered
  today* from *does not exist*.
- Follow the books entirely — no count, no empty heading.

**Why yes:** it shows the player the house's actual slate, which the real books destroy, and it makes
the contents list a fixed-length object rather than a shape-shifter.

## 3 — NAVIGATION. How does he find offer #60 of ~80?

**Measured: 54px row pitch, ~7.8 rows per screen.** At that pitch the full vocabulary is **~10
screens, not the four** the research arithmetic predicted. Nine destinations at six-plus rows each
means most tabs scroll. **Call 5 confirms this figure rather than shrinking it.**

- **Yes — printed folio plus a contents block.** *(recommend)* The rail prints `46–66 of 80` — a
  fact, not a scrollbar hint — and a contents list (group, line range, count) replaces a search
  field. Worst case **three taps to any market, against ~7 on DraftKings**.
- Contents block only, no page numbers.
- Neither — the rail stays what it is today (it works; verified scrolling at 1.88× on PLAYERS).

**Why yes:** orientation is the most durable complaint in the whole category, and **we have no
Ctrl+F** — desktop books free-ride on it, which is why none of them ships market-level search. This
replaces a missing platform capability in a register native to paper. Nobody has ever complained
that a racecard is hard to navigate.

## 4 — THE PRICE. Two halves, and I recommend splitting them

The build **already** leads with the market name and sets the price as plain right-aligned type. The
inversion I proposed is mostly already there — it just was never ruled. **It arrived by migration
drift, not by decision.**

- **Half one: ratify the name-first hierarchy as a ruling.** *(recommend yes, now)* Free — it records
  what is built. Worth having because once a board is built price-first it is not cheaply reversed,
  and today nothing stops it drifting back.
- **Half two: does the price take the amber?** *(recommend: decide on a frame, not here)* The law
  says amber = money, and a price is money — that argues yes. Against: **~80 amber prices on one
  sheet**, and amber is also the *action* colour, so if everything is amber nothing is. That is a
  read at distance, and this seat has been wrong twice this week predicting instead of measuring.
  One sheet rendered both ways settles it in an afternoon. **If you want it decided now: I lean
  yes** — and call 5 strengthens that lean, because at one offer per row the amber lands in a single
  column down the right edge, which reads as an annotation rail rather than as scattered ink.

## 5 — ROW DENSITY. Ratify one offer per row, or restore the two-up pairs?

**What changed, and what nobody recorded.** Pre-migration, `OVER`/`UNDER` shared one row two-up. At
HEAD every offer takes its own full-width row. **Six offers took three rows and now take six.**
Nothing in the register records that as a decision — **that much is a real defect whichever way you
rule**, because an unrecorded halving is exactly the kind of thing a later hand "restores".

**I flagged this as a probable regression. Checking canon says it probably is not, and I am
withdrawing that framing.** The studio has ruled **one-per-row four separate times**, each time for
the same reason:

- **`S74`** — a matchup is **NOT** one row with two price cells; it is two stacked lines, **the price
  on the same line as the side it belongs to**.
- **`T88`** — one option per row gives each **its own cost, its own key, and somewhere for its
  affordance to live**.
- **`T92`** — compact forms, one per row. · **`T74`** — one leg per row.

**The form at HEAD is in step with all four. The two-up form was the odd one out.**

- **Ratify one offer per row.** *(recommend)* Records the build as a decision and gives it reasons.
- **Restore the two-up pairs.** Halves the two-way tabs. Against four standing precedents, and it
  puts two offers under one line.
- **A third form — the line named once, both prices beside it** (`1.5 GOALS · OVER +120 · UNDER
  −140`). Denser still, and it names the line once rather than twice. **But `S74` rejected exactly
  this shape**: the price belongs on the line of the thing it prices.

**Why ratify, in three:**

1. **Four standing precedents, one reasoning.** Reversing here would make the market sheet the only
   surface in the studio that does it differently.
2. **The empty width is not waste — it is the annotation gap.** A ~996px row carrying `OVER 1.5` and
   a price looks sparse beside a sportsbook, and that gap **is the racecard idiom** — the same device
   as call 2's `CORNERS ….. 11` leader. Closing it would make the sheet read as a table.
3. **The rows we need do not come from doubling up.** Measured: nine destinations × ~7.8 rows ≈ **70
   slots against ~80 offers**, and Correct Score alone wants 12–16. The answer is **more destinations
   plus scrolling inside them** — already ruled (`S25-am`) and demonstrated (PLAYERS at 1.88×).
   Doubling up buys about one screen and costs a precedent.

**Correcting my own flag, on the record.** I raised this as *"the cheapest source of the rows the
nine homeless kinds need."* **It is not** — the rows come from destinations and scrolling. And it
does **not** interact with call 4 the way I said: **80 offers is 80 prices either way**, so the amber
*volume* is identical and only its arrangement changes.
