1 · The Annotated Form Guide

You do not tap a button — you mark up the form. The slate is a dense betting paper being worked over at 2 a.m. in a cheap reader's night mode, and every pick is a mark left on it: selection is a biro ring drawn round the price, not a pill that lights up. The house printed the document; the right-hand margin is his. Wax-pencil amber is reserved for money and the primary action, and oxide red appears only where the house stamps something. Loud register: the sheet stops being his to mark and starts being marked at him.

2 · The Catalogue Sleeve

A record-label catalogue identity: lifted matte black, a white hairline plot as body ink, catalogue numbers as the only headline, vast unprinted ground, small engraved caps used sparingly, and the active entry alone decoded to full colour. A sleeve is a personal object he owns, and the register is calm, dark and precise. Its native cipher — colour strips standing in for words — was dropped in fusion, because every price and reason on this product must stay literal.
