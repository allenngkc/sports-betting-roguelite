## 2. THE TWO GATES, ASSERTED

Both were unassertable under (B) — the clock runs backwards there by design, and the ending line
fires once per leg. Under (A) they are the ruling's own test.

1. **`RENDERED CLOCK NON-DECREASING`**, across a ticket's whole sweat, **per fixture.** This is
   `T135`'s own measurement turned into a gate, and it is a string comparison on frames the harness
   already logs. **It is the falsifier for arm A**: if it fails, the restructure did not land.
2. **`THE MATCH ENDS LEVEL` COUNTED PER (TICKET, FIXTURE) == 1** — not per leg. Counted, not
   sampled.

**Both gates must be written to fail loudly on a MULTI-fixture ticket too**, where the correct count
is one per fixture and the clock legitimately resets between them. A gate that reads "per ticket"
instead of "per (ticket, fixture)" would fail a correct multi-fixture broadcast — the exact
over-reach the spec's §3.1 was corrected for at `T140-am`.

## 3. THE THREE PHASES

### PHASE 1 — the engine's session/path restructure · **ESCALATION**

`engine/` is **read-only to the TV lane**, so this is not executable there at all. **It needs an
engine-owning lane.** Design input in §4.

**Already ruled, and the lane should not be asked to re-derive any of it:**

- **The telling contract** — `T140`.
- **Grades land in LEG ORDER, after ONE hold** — `T87-am2`'s hold is unchanged: N legs, N grades,
  **one hold, not N** (spec §3.2). *Leg order means ticket order, not resolution order.*
- **The pending-loss window** — `T143`: it **opens ONCE PER WHISTLE, after every grade on that
  fixture has landed**, never once per dead leg, because N windows is N interruptions and that is
  the rewind's cousin. It **names every leg that died at that whistle**, and where more than one
  died it states that **no single call saves the ticket BEFORE the offer is presented** (`S85`).
- **The cash-out** — `T143`: a **TICKET-level** fact and always was. **No leg's probability is ever
  shown alone.**

**OWED BEFORE THIS PHASE STARTS: two rulings, §4.**

### PHASE 2 — the count ledger, N-live · **needs NO new ruling**

**The design rule already exists and was pre-committed for exactly this event.** `T115-am`
(batch 109) deferred concurrent live count legs as *"NOT REACHABLE, SO NOT BUILT — the engine
forbids two legs on one matchup and legs sweat sequentially"* — **and named the principle for the
day that changed:**

> *"the scene takes the **SMALLEST distance across live legs**, which is the hand-over falling out
> of the same rule."*

**`T142` struck that premise.** The engine does **not** forbid two legs on one matchup;
`SameMatchModel`, `SameMatchPrice` and `Ticket.SameMatch` all ship, and SGP is a delivered feature.
**So the deferral's condition has fired: the principle stands, unamended, and this phase is a BUILD
rather than a ruling.**

**What may NOT vary without a new ruling** (`T115-am`, unchanged): the two-rung stepped shape, the
state-based quiet, the ticket-derived valence, and the exclusion of the clock from the trigger. The
threshold's *value* is tuning and needs nothing.

### PHASE 3 — presentation: the live set, and re-pointed pins

**`T142` reduces this phase and the reduction should be priced in rather than rediscovered:** the
ticket column is **already N-live-capable by construction** — it reads legs as a collection and
checks each row's own live state independently, never a hard-coded active-leg index, and its author
recorded why.

**Already ruled:**

- **The pulse** — `DESIGN.md` §9: one pulse *kind* exists, `LIVE`, and **when several legs are live
  they all pulse IN PHASE off one shared clock**, so the surface reads as one system breathing.
  *That shared phase is also what makes concurrency legible.* Nothing to author.
- **`NEXT` leaves the legs on this fixture** — spec §3.2.

**OWED BEFORE THIS PHASE STARTS: one ruling — the leg counter, §4.3.**

## 4. WHAT THE ENGINE-OWNING LANE NEEDS FROM DESIGN

**Four things are ruled and only need restating as a contract** — §3's phase-1 list above:
the telling contract, grade order and the single hold, the pending-loss window's shape, and
cash-out as a ticket-level fact.

**Two things are NOT ruled, and this seat owes them before phase 1 starts:**

### 4.1 THE PROSE ANCHOR UNDER N LIVE LEGS — owed

`SweatFlavor.PickedHomeForPresentation` answers *"which team the prose anchors on"* — **expressly
not which side was backed** — and returns one side **per leg**. Under one telling with N legs live
on a fixture, **the flavour has N candidate anchors and no rule for choosing.**

This is not `K17` (the marker being wrong for non-moneyline kinds, already flagged) and not
`T152-am` (nothing on the row carrying the team). It is the new question arm A creates.
**Owed from this seat.**

### 4.2 THE DISPLAYED WIN-PROBABILITY'S SEED — owed

`T143` named this seam and **deliberately did not rule it**: `_liveProb` is seeded **per leg**
(`SweatSession.cs:265`, `_ticket.Legs[_currentLeg].TrueProb`) and `RevealedView.Reset` seeds
`WinProbability` from `Legs[0].TrueProb`. `T143`'s words: *"On a sequential single-live model that
is arguable; under N-live it is a **visible lie**."*

**Arm A makes it N-live. The seam becomes a defect on the day phase 1 lands**, and `T143` explicitly
reserved the single-leg case as its own question. **Owed from this seat.**

### 4.3 THE LEG COUNTER — owed, and COUPLED TO `T91-cl`

`LEG n/m` in the scorebug band says which leg is live. **Under arm A a fixture can carry several
legs live at once, so "which leg" stops being a single number.** No register row governs this
element — searched at batch 158: *leg counter*, *LEG 4/4*, *LEG {n}* return **zero rows**.

**And it is already under a separate ruling that wants it moved.** `T91-cl` measured the counter's
ink overprinting the scoreline by **41.7px**, ruled the 2px floor binds them, and showed that a
clearance rule alone cannot fix it — **569.0px available against 583.3px needed, a 14.3px deficit.**
This seat recommended moving the counter out of the band and left the geometry to TV.

**LAND THEM TOGETHER.** Arm A is re-thinking what the counter SAYS at the same moment `T91-cl`
requires its position to change. Doing them separately means moving the element twice, and the
second move re-opens a measurement the first one just settled.
