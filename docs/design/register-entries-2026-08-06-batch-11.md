## S62 — `TICKET 1.0` — zero-indexed, decimal-ambiguous ticket identity. **RULED — violation.**

**NEW · DD 2026-08-06.** On `15-ledger-across-rounds` the two settled tickets print **`TICKET 1.0`** and
**`TICKET 2.0`**, while the leg sub-rows directly beneath them count **`1.` `2.` `3.`** and the form's

Two defects in one string:

1. **Zero-indexed identity on a player-facing printed form.** `1.0` is round 1, ticket 0. The array

**The design system already carries the notation and it is unambiguous:** `LedgerEntry`'s spec form is
**`R2 · TICKET 02`** — round named, ticket named, both one-indexed, both zero-padded to match the
form's own entries. Use it.

the lead built to prove something else.** Every round-1 ledger prints `1.0` and reads as a plausible

## M1 — Markets: BTTS is structurally unreachable. **RULED — G7's population narrows; the exclusion is named, measured and expiring.**

**NEW · DD 2026-08-06.** The design question inside Allen's arm-B tables, and mine to answer.

**The lead is right and the gate is measuring the wrong thing for this market.**

BTTS is a near-even two-way market. Its odds never clear the Longshot Photo's ≥3.0 threshold, and under
exact two-way de-vig every selection ties at −vig, so it can never *strictly* win a tie. **No tie-break
can reach it.** That is arithmetic, not tuning.

**Separate what G7 conflates.** Two different requirements have been collapsed into one red light:

- **"Every priced offer is reachable on the surface"** — C19, a design law. BTTS is on ENTRY, priced,
  selectable, and reachable by the player. **C19 is satisfied and was never at risk.**
- **"The skilled bot exercises this market"** — a balance property. And a sharp declining a near-even
  market where he has no edge is **the bot being correct**, not the bot being broken. G7 is asserting a
  requirement the design never made.

**Ruling.** G7's population is markets a skilled bot could select **given an edge to find**. BTTS has no
edge to find under the current pricing model, so it leaves the population — and the gate's own line
states the exclusion, its reason and its boundary.

**This is an R23-am exclusion, not an S51 one, and the distinction is the whole point.** R23-am was
authorised because the excluded region was named, measured and sanctioned by an existing law. S51's was
refused because it merely went green. This one is named (BTTS), measured (the lead's tie analysis and
the coverage tables), and structural (exact de-vig, not a preference). **It removes a false red, not a
true one.**

**Expiry, per C16.** The exclusion **lapses the moment the v2 pricing-noise model lands.** That model
gives the bot a genuine edge to find, BTTS re-enters the population, and G7 covers it again without
anyone remembering to re-open it.

**Refused explicitly, so it is not proposed later:** do **not** manufacture an item that rewards short
odds in order to make BTTS attractive. The lead named this himself — *"manufacturing a reason for the
bot to take BTTS purely to turn G7 green would be tuning the instrument to pass"* — and declined it.
**He was right, and he was right for the same reason he was right at S51.** That is twice this lane has
refused a green light it could have taken.

**On arm B itself — Allen's call, and I take no part in it.** One design-relevant observation for his
decision: an ordering bias that made **two shipped markets invisible to every balance gate in the
studio** is the same defect family as the five vacuous greens. Corners and cards were not
under-selected; they were **unselectable, at every seed, for every build**, and no gate could see it.
The instrument was blind, not the bot.

## C32 — A gate states its resolution. **LAW.**

**Ruled · DD 2026-08-06.** Promoted from the markets lead's significance check, which is the third
distinct instrument law this studio has been handed by a lead rather than a seat.

**A gate reports the smallest difference it can resolve, alongside its acceptance band. A band narrower
than about twice the instrument's noise is not a gate.**

The case, in the lead's own numbers: G3's acceptance band is **5–8%**, three points wide. At
`--runs 1000` a win rate near 6% carries **±0.75pp** standard error. The instrument therefore cannot
distinguish a real one-point regression from a coin toss — **and the "before" figure itself moved 6.2%
→ 5.5% purely by increasing n.** A gate that cannot fail for the thing it exists to catch is the
vacuous-green family in its subtlest form yet: not blind, just too coarse to see.

Consequences, standing:

1. **Any "the gates moved" claim at n=1000 requires an n=10,000 confirmation before it is believed —
   in either direction.** Including a claim that they did *not* move.
2. A gate whose band is inside its own noise is **re-scoped or re-instrumented**, not read.
3. This joins C25 (what it cannot see) and C28 (what produced a verdict) as the third reporting axis:
   **scope, coverage, resolution.**

Endorsed without reservation: checking the significance rather than reporting the 0.8pp drop, then
**re-measuring the baseline too and finding it had moved as much as the change.** Reporting that your
own control was noisy is the least comfortable and most useful sentence in the submission.
