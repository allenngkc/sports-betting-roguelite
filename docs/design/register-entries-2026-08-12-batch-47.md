## T86-am — T16's ban reaches, but not by extension. The principle underneath it is ruled general

TV reports the `(99%)` figure is **the same quantity class as T16's subject at a different site** — a
leg's probability at a decision prompt rather than on the momentum tape — and asks whether T16 reaches
beyond the tape.

**It does. But "does ruling X reach site Y" is exactly the question S67 was written about** — *a ruling
can only reach the call sites that route through the thing it was ruled on.* T16 was ruled on the
tape. Extending it by analogy is the move S67 warns against, and the honest way to cover the second
site is to **rule the principle directly and name T16 as its first instance**, not to stretch T16.

### The principle

> **The match theatre prints facts and offers. It does not print opinions.**
>
> A **price** is an offer — a thing the house is standing behind, which the player can transact
> against. A **probability** is the house's opinion about an outcome, with nothing attached to it. The
> player can take or leave a price. He can only agree or disagree with an opinion, and this surface
> does not ask him to do that.

**This explains T16, T23 and T32(4) in one line** — win-probability numeral out, backed-player numeral
out, the 10px numeral deleted — where before there were three separate deletions that happened to
rhyme. T16's own second reason is this principle already: *locked odds make the read the player's
job.*

**So `(99%)` goes**, at the decision prompt as on the tape, and for the same reason: it is the
machine's read, handed to the player, in a game whose skill is forming that read himself.

### The constructive half — what the prompt gets instead

The counter-argument deserves an answer rather than a refusal: **at a frozen moment with three
options, a player with no information is flipping a coin, not deciding.** True.

**The answer is that the basis for the decision should be an OFFER, not an OPINION.** If `SEND TO
REVIEW` costs something, print **the cost** — that is a fact he transacts against, it belongs on this
surface, and it makes the choice a real one. `SEND TO REVIEW — $40` is a decision. `SEND TO REVIEW
(99%)` is an instruction wearing a number.

Everything else he needs is already on screen and is his to read: the score, the clock, his legs,
their prices, the state.

### Routed, not ruled — two things visible from here

- **A 99% option contains no decision.** Whatever the prompt costs, an option that succeeds ~always is
  pressed ~always. That is a balance question for the lane that owns the mechanic, not a design
  ruling, and it is named only because it was visible in the string.
- **Three key-bound commit actions on the TV sit against T22's boundary** — *commit is an act on the
  laptop; the TV's gesture is a preview, and release is never confirm.* If `InterventionPrompt` is a
  new mechanic that genuinely commits from the theatre, **it needs its own ruling rather than
  inheriting T22's**, because T22 drew that line deliberately. **Flagged, not ruled** — the mechanic
  is not in evidence at this seat and nothing here blocks on it.
