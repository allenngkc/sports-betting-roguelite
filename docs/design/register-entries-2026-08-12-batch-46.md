## First: my batch-45 DIAGNOSIS is superseded. The rulings are not

Batch 45 diagnosed *two superimposed effects* — correlation shortening, **vig lengthening
everything** — and built the learnability problem on their sum. That was correct for the state
described in `step-1-recommendation.md` (78.07% of two-leg tickets lengthen at `Ω_sgp = Ω`).

**The adopted margin rule removes the vig half.** The brief states it exactly:
`shown price ÷ product = 1/ρ`, so **51.4% of two-leg tickets are exactly independent and price
IDENTICALLY to the multiplied number.** There is no general lengthening left to explain.

Recorded as a **moving input, not a misreading** — the dial was set after the document I ruled on.
But the consequence matters and is stated plainly: **the situation is now much better than batch 45
described, and every ruling in it survives on stronger ground.** The divergence is no longer a
mixture; it is a clean signal that appears **only where correlation exists**, which is exactly the
condition clause 4 was written to require.

**One thing gets harder, and it should be named.** Intermittent correctness teaches a habit more
strongly than uniform divergence would: the player multiplies, is **exactly right 51.4% of the time**,
and is then silently wrong by ~39% on the shapes he most wants to build. A rule that works half the
time is learned as a rule that works.

## Q1 — Does the player see the correlation? **Yes. The qualitative marker. Option 3**

**Ruled: the mark, no arithmetic** — S73 clause 2 confirmed against the sharper numbers.

**Why not "show nothing":** the brief makes the case itself — *a player who multiplies three legs off
the board and gets a bigger number than the slip shows has found a real discrepancy and deserves an
answer.* Silence there is a cost the player cannot see at the point of spending, which is **S17's
class**.

**Why not "show both numbers with the correction named":** it makes the slip a calculator (**T21** —
values appear when the sim emits them, never computed in presentation), it renders the same fact
twice (**S58**), and it puts a number visibly moving on the document. Note it would not always be a
*deduction* — negatively correlated tickets pay **more** — but a two-directional correction line is
worse, not better: it teaches the player to watch a number change rather than to read his own ticket.

**What the mark buys, and this is the whole argument:** with it, the player's complete and *correct*
mental model needs no arithmetic at all —

> **unmarked legs multiply; marked legs pay less.**

That is true, it is learnable, and it is exactly the rule the brief says is available. **The mark is
what makes the multiplication rule work** rather than what replaces it — it tells him precisely when
his own arithmetic applies.

**On the fairness fact — do NOT print it.** The brief offers that a same-game ticket carries the same
house edge as an equivalent cross-game parlay, *"if the surface makes any promise about fairness, that
one is true and defensible."* It is true, and it stays off the slip: **a house that explains its own
fairness at the point of spending reads as a house with something to explain.** S44's boundary is the
precedent — the machine does not wear the house's brand, and the house does not testify about itself
on the player's document. The mark answers the question; the promise would only raise it.

*(Where that fact should live — a rules surface, the ledger — is not ruled here. It is worth keeping
available; it is not worth saying unprompted.)*

## Q2 — Redundant combinations: **WARN. Not block, not silence**

**Silence is refused outright.** The brief's own words: *the player can be quietly milked by a trap
they cannot see.* That is **S17 verbatim** — a cost invisible at the moment money moves — and it is
the single most corrosive thing this product could do, because its whole promise is that the numbers
do not lie.

**Blocking is refused, and not for the reason the brief offers.** Paternalism is the weaker argument.
The real one: **a redundant leg is a legal bet, correctly priced.** This game is about a player
learning to read a book. **A house that prevents you from being stupid is not this product; a house
that tells you and lets you do it anyway is exactly this product.** Blocking also forecloses uses
nobody has anticipated — a relic, a challenge condition, a deliberate shape.

**The clutter objection dissolves on inspection.** The warning appears on **22 shapes**, not on every
slip. It costs nothing on a slip that does not contain one.

**The copy states the fact, in the machine's voice, naming both legs:**

> `OVER 1.5 GOALS adds nothing — OVER 2.5 GOALS already contains it.`

This is S73 clause 6, now confirmed with the fork resolved. **It is also the most credible thing in
the feature**, because it costs the house money to say — which is precisely what makes the rest of the
machine's statements believable.

## Q3 — Impossible combinations, ruled DRAWS-AWARE. The trap does not partially dissolve; it goes

Allen greenlit draws today. **Both artefact shapes were impossible only because 1–1 was
unrepresentable.** With draws in the model, `BTTS YES + Under 2.5` is **a legal, ordinary, popular
ticket** — and so is the other. They are not refused, no copy is written for them, and there is
nothing left to expose.

**The standing rule this produces, which is bigger than the two shapes:**

> **The surface refuses only what is impossible in FOOTBALL — never what is merely impossible in the
> model. Where the model is more restrictive than the game it simulates, the MODEL is the defect, and
> the refusal does not ship.**

**Why this is a design law and not a copy preference.** A refusal is the machine making a factual
claim about the world. If it says *these cannot both happen* about something that happens in real
football every weekend, a football-literate player catches the product lying — and **every other
number the machine shows loses its credit at the same moment.** This surface's entire authority is
that it does not lie about facts. That authority is indivisible.

**It generalises past draws, which is why it is written as a rule.** No-draws was one v1 constraint;
any other simplification will manufacture the same false refusals. **Before a refusal ships, it is
checked against football, not against the model** — and the check states what it could not cover
(C18 §4.2).

**Step-3 consequence, and it is the build reach the brief predicted:** the impossible set must be
**re-derived after draws land.** The 22 two-leg and 57 three-leg shapes were enumerated against a
drawless model; that enumeration is now stale in the direction that matters — **it over-refuses.**
Every shape it lists is re-checked, and the two known artefacts are expected to leave the set.

**What the refusal reads like** — S73 clause 5, unchanged and now unconstrained:

> `OVER 2.5 GOALS and UNDER 1.5 GOALS cannot both land.`

The fact, both legs named, once, at the slip, in the slip's own space. **Not a modal** (this surface
has no takeovers — S55), **not a disabled control** (S24 bans the disabled state; S56 bans a
distinction in a channel the player cannot see), **no scolding** — S71/S72's voice law: name the
state, not the owner; second person only in genuine imperatives.

It teaches because it is **specific**: the player learns which two legs conflict, and that is real
football knowledge he keeps.

## The ink split, stated because three annotations now exist and they will get muddled

| annotation | ink | why |
|---|---|---|
| the correlation link between two picks | **oxide** | the house's mark **on the player's picks** — S3's own definition, the dead-leg strike's family |
| `adds nothing — X already contains it` | **toner** | the machine **stating a fact in its own space** — S26's form, *state rather than exhort* |

**Biro is never used for any of them.** Biro is only ever what the player chose (S3), and S26 is the
worked case of getting this exactly wrong.

The distinction that keeps oxide from diluting: **oxide marks the house acting on his picks; toner is
the machine saying something true.** A refusal is not the house acting — it is arithmetic, spoken.
