## `C51` → `C52` — the collision resolved

**The collision.** Batch 97 promoted *a disclosure block that is not re-verified per dock becomes a
false claim* as `C51`. Batch 64 had already given `C51` to *a cross-element invariant is an
ASSERTION or it does not exist* — four weeks earlier, and in force.

**Why it was silent, which is the part worth keeping.** The C-series ran 1–51 with **no gaps**. A
promotion that takes "the next number" without checking the last one looks exactly like a correct
one, and there was no empty slot anywhere in the series to make the mistake visible. Nothing in the
register's shape could have caught it; only a reader comparing two tables could, and nobody had
reason to until Allen's re-filing instruction sent someone to look at the destination table.

**The resolution.** `C22.1` — *where the same finding is ruled twice under two IDs, the earlier ID
governs*. The letter of that clause covers one finding under two IDs and this is its inverse, two
findings under one ID, but the principle points the same way and the earlier law is also the one
already cited elsewhere. **Batch 64's law keeps `C51`. Batch 97's law becomes `C52`.**

Authorised as bookkeeping on the orchestrator's relay under that named precedent, and logged to
Allen. The seat did **not** take this on its own word at batch 98, and was right not to: a law's ID
is what a lead cites, which makes a renumber different in kind from moving a row between tables.
What made it cheap was that nothing had been built against it — three citations, one week old.

## Recorded

**The law that got renumbered is the law about this.** `C52` says a disclosure block copied without
re-checking stops being a disclosure and becomes a false claim. A law ID taken without re-checking
the series is the same move, and it landed on `C52`'s own row — the batch that promoted the rule
broke it in the act of promoting it.

**And the near-miss is the finding.** This surfaced only because an instruction to re-file a row
forced a look at the destination table. A collision nobody looks for is a collision that survives,
and the register had no way to surface it: **there is no check in this studio that would have
caught a duplicate ID.** There is now, and it runs at every seating alongside the render audit and
the cell-count scan.
