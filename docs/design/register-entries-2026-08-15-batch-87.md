### 2 — THE SUBSTANTIVE RULING, and §8 supplies it

> **§8: the theatre prints FACTS and OFFERS.**

**A price cell is both at once** — the house's line on an outcome (a fact) and a thing he may take
(an offer). **At the cap it is still true and no longer takeable.**

> **RULED: at the leg cap, an unavailable price STOPS BEING AN OFFER AND REMAINS A FACT. The offer
> affordance goes; the price stays legible.**

**That is the whole treatment in one sentence, and it is derived rather than chosen** — the surface's
own first distinction, applied to a control that has changed category.

### 3 — Why `frozen`'s dim may not be reused, sharper than S2 alone

S2 bars it. **The meanings say why, and the why is the part worth keeping:**

| | what it means | what it does to the price |
|---|---|---|
| `frozen` | **the round is locked** — the price is no longer live information | **dims the FACT** |
| the cap | **your slip is full** — the price is still true | **removes the OFFER** |

**Two different meanings, and one treatment for both would teach him that a full slip is a closed
round.** **The distinction is not a nicety; it is which of the two things about a price has changed.**

**And the panel is as empty as it was.** The table occupies the top quarter; roughly two-thirds of

> **RULED: the emptiness is not the state. It is the design. The panel is sized to the SCREEN, not

### Ruled, and the remedy is the surface's own legal move

> **The panel is sized ONCE AT DESIGN TIME to the content it carries.**

**The argument in one line: a surface that takes the entire stage and returns three rows has not
earned the stage.** **This is not a complaint about empty space** — this studio has twice ruled
emptiness correct (the desktop's 99.7%-at-ground, S52; the draws block's honest empty line). **The
objection is the EXCHANGE**: he gives up the ticket column, the pitch and the scorebug, and receives
a table that would fit in a quarter of what it took.
