### Ruled: route (2). The accepted treatment moves into the slot.

**§6.1 already specifies it** — *"accepted: brief L4 punch, then `CASHED OUT $x` at L3"* — and the
build diverged. **The divergence is what created the problem**, so the fix is to close it rather than
compensate for it.

Verified: an L3 gold field against `goldInk` computes to **9.68 : 1**, and the same mechanism at L4 is
already shipped and measured at **7.95 : 1**. The route reuses an inversion that is built, verified and
sitting one state away.

**Refused, and why, so neither returns:**

- **(1) interpolate the ink against the flood alpha.** It solves the contrast and creates a worse
  problem: **the money word changes colour mid-beat**, a motion this surface does not have. §7 is
  quantised — *a brightness level swaps in a discrete step* — and a continuous hue lerp on the payout
  figure is the laptop's laid-ink register on the wrong screen. It also leaves the ground moving and
  compensates for it, which is the shape T68 was.
- **(3) the text takes its own stable ground.** A new opaque ground under the payout is a fourth
  surface value invented to host one string, and §6.1 already provides the field.

**T43 is not in tension with this.** *"Nothing of the offer outlives the accept"* means the **offer** —
the amount, the `HOLD E` instruction, the actionable field. The slot as a rectangle is the surface's
own furniture and §6.1 gives it six states, of which `accepted` is one. Hiding the offer and reusing
the slot are the same act.

**The flood stays.** It is the payoff's celebration ground and nothing here touches it — it simply
stops being the thing a money figure has to be legible against.

## C34 [transcribed as C35] — an element and its ground must not move together. **LAW.**

**Ruled · DD 2026-08-08.** Promoted from the lead's §3, which named the general form of T68 more
clearly than T68 did.

`ApplyBoost`'s `Payout` case drives `_bigAmountHdrMat` **and** `_goldFloodHdrMat`. So `RequestL4` boosts
the text and the ground behind it together and **their ratio is preserved** — brightness cannot
separate them at any amplitude.

**Where an element and the surface behind it are driven by one control, no brightness change can make
the element readable.** Legibility comes from the ground being *stable* and the ink being *inverted
against it*, never from boosting the pair.

This is the third correction to the same instrument family:

- **C33-am2** — a dominance gate is silent on legibility.
- **C33-am3** — a contrast ratio is computed in linear space.
- **C34 [C35]** — a boost that drives both sides of a contrast is not a legibility lever.

**Gate V8 extends:** an inverting element reports **whether its own ground is static across the beat**.
A moving ground makes the reported ratio an instant, not a state — and this defect would have shown up
as a passing number taken at the wrong alpha.
