### RULED — the remedy is CONJUNCTIVE and the copy must read as one

**This is the whole of the ruling and everything else follows from it.** The lane's own two binding
rules say **spending only the first element leaves the slip refused**, and the set must be removed in

**BANNED, verbatim: `or`, `either`, `one of`, `any of` in a remedy.** English's natural form for a
list of fixes is disjunctive, and **the model's truth is conjunctive** — so the idiomatic phrasing is
the wrong one, and reaching for it ships a remedy that does not work. **The player follows it, drops
one, and the slip is still refused.**

**That failure is worse than no remedy at all.** S73-am4's requirement is a *verified* remedy — a
refusal that names a fix which does not fix it is the house giving an instruction that fails, at the
point of spending, which is S17's own subject. **A remedy is a promise about what will happen when he
does it.**

**The remedy names every member of the set, and reads as one act:** *drop* **A**, **B** *and* **C** —
never *drop A or B or C*.

conflict cannot use it, and the sentence has no honest degradation — *"cannot both land"* over three
legs is simply wrong.

**RULED: the cause is authored for the plural in the same cut** — `… cannot all land` for three or
more, `… cannot both land` retained for exactly two. **Two authored forms, chosen by arity, never one

never shrinks*, and the same principle forbids inflecting a sentence by string surgery.

**The lane must state the CAUSE set's arity**, which the correction gave only for remedies. If the

## S76 — THE VOIDED TICKET IN THE LEDGER. Both falsehoods confirmed; VOID takes its own state row.

**surething-ui's reading is CONFIRMED, and the citation is corrected in its favour — S41 is stronger
than the report claims.**

### The em dash: S41 already forbade it, two years of reasoning ago

**S41 is not "the token for a genuinely unknowable amount."** S41 is the ruling that **expired**
S36's designed absence:

> the figure prints (wax, paired WON); **the margin's RETURNED total prints the sum and never an em
> dash**; **if an unknowable record ever exists, the total prints the known sum and the record's own
> cell carries the absence.**

**So the em dash was already confined to a record's own cell, and only for a genuinely unknowable
amount.** A voided ticket fails that test twice over: **the return is the stake, returned in full and
unconditionally** — *"the ticket voids IN FULL and the stake is returned unconditionally"* — which
makes it **the most exactly-known figure on the surface**, not an unknowable one.

**RULED: the em dash comes off. The figure prints the known sum.** No new ruling was needed; **the
build never implemented S41 here.**

### `OPEN` is the second falsehood and it is the worse one

`Voided` is a **terminal** state. The engine models it as distinct from Won and Lost **precisely so
that no consumer books a refund as a loss** — the Bad Beat Jar, the Scar and the run's tally all
depend on that. **Printing `OPEN` over a settled ticket says the money is still in play when it is
back in his bank.** **RULED: it comes off.**

### RULED — VOID is a third terminal STATE. It is not a third RESULT, and C47 does not forbid it.

**This will be argued with, so it is settled here.** C47 rules that **result language needs no third
word** — *the match has three outcomes; a bet has two*. **C47 governs the DRAW, and a void is not a
draw's result.** The owning doc already says so in the same breath:

> A market that *returns the stake* on a draw is a **VOID**, which the enum already carries — **a
> different market's rule, never a third result.**

**A void is not the bet resolving a third way. It is the bet not having happened.** Inventing `DREW`
would model the match inside the bet's vocabulary; **naming a returned stake does not**, and refusing
to name it forces a settled ticket to wear `GREEN`, `DEAD` or a lie. **All three are worse.**

### The §3.3 row, and the one negative that matters most

§3.3's outcome vocabulary is **`GREEN` / `DEAD`** — two terminal words, and **the table has no row for
a void.** It gets one, under the section's first law: **status is never colour alone**, so the row
carries a **word** plus a mark.

**THE BINDING NEGATIVE: a void NEVER takes the oxide strike.** The strike is `DEAD`'s mark and oxide
is the house's mark. **A struck-through voided ticket reads as a loss — which is the exact confusion
the engine spends a whole enum value to prevent**, undone at the last surface.

**CANDIDATE, and it wants frames before it is verified (C11):** the void reads as **the entry rubbed
out** — the rub-out is already in this surface's vocabulary and it is what an annotated form guide
actually does to a bet that did not stand. **The word `VOID` prints** (S22 — the role prints as a
word), the **stake figure prints as the known sum**, and **the row does not drain to `DEAD`'s .55**,
because nothing was lost.

**Not ruled from this seat: how it reads.** No frame of a voided ledger row exists. **The vocabulary,
the negatives and the state's existence are ruled above and are buildable now; the treatment is a
candidate and takes Allen's approval on frames.**
