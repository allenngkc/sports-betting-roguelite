## Method, and what it cannot see (C18 §4.2)

Every one of the 343 rows was probed on its **leading verdict plus the tail of its amendment
chain** — the two places the register puts current status. 17 rows were then read at **full
length** before being reported here. Nothing was filtered out before the probe, so there is no
row this sweep never looked at.

**Blind spot, stated:** where a row's current status sits *mid-amendment* rather than at its
tail, this sweep will have mis-read it. That is a real risk on rows with long chains. Two
keyword passes were attempted first and **both were discarded as unusable** — 52 hits, then 90,
overwhelmingly false, because these rows are 2–9k characters of dense prose in which every
marker word appears somewhere. Matching cannot classify this register; reading can.

**A stale-head warning that applies throughout:** a row's *opening* verdict is its ORIGINAL
verdict and is frequently overtaken by its own amendments. `S82` still opens **WITH ALLEN**
though Allen ruled it at batch 77. `T85` opens *"the value is UNRULED"* though its clause closes
totally further down. **Read the tail, not the head.** Several rows below are here *because*
their heads are stale.
