## S78 — THE SEVEN SENTENCES. Six stand. One is a regression against copy that already exists.

### Drafts 2–4 — APPROVED as written, and they are a FAMILY

```
SharedScoreline  reinforcing  THE SAME GOALS SETTLE BOTH.
                 opposing     THE SAME GOALS SETTLE THESE OPPOSITE WAYS.
ScorerOfSide     reinforcing  THE SAME TEAM'S GOALS SETTLE BOTH.
                 opposing     THE SAME TEAM'S GOALS SETTLE THESE OPPOSITE WAYS.
SharedCount      corner       THE SAME CORNERS SETTLE BOTH. / … THESE OPPOSITE WAYS.
                 card         THE SAME CARDS SETTLE BOTH.   / … THESE OPPOSITE WAYS.
```

**RULED: a family, and deliberately. Do not re-author them apart.**

**The shape is not a template applied to save effort — the shape IS the claim.** Every one of these
relations is literally *one shared thing settles both legs*; the sentences differ **exactly where the
relations differ** (the shared thing) and are identical **exactly where the relations are identical**.
**That is what distinguishes a family from templating: the variation tracks the meaning.**

**And the payoff is the player's, not the author's.** Canon already fixes his correct mental model as
one idea — *unmarked legs multiply; marked legs pay less.* **A family means that after the first
encounter he reads only the DIFFERENCE**; four idioms would make him re-parse a whole sentence to
learn a thing he already knows. **Four shapes for one idea is the cost, and there is no benefit
against it.**

**`GOALS` stands, and `SCORELINE` was considered and refused** — it is closer to the relation's
internal name and it would avoid presupposing goals, but **`GOALS / CORNERS / CARDS` is a clean triple
of countable match events and that parallelism is what makes the family read as one.** The
presupposition is theoretical: the sentence is made at slip time about the settlement mechanism, and
*the same goals settle both* is read as *goals, however many* — never as a prediction that goals will
come.

**Sign is carried, and carrying it was correct.** Reinforcing and opposing are opposite claims about
the same shared thing; **one sentence per relation would have stated one of them falsely about the
other.** Seven sentences for four relations is the honest count.

### Draft 1 — `Implies` — NOT APPROVED. It collides with an existing ruling and drops the reason.

> Drafted: **ONE OF THESE ALREADY COVERS THE OTHER.**

**`surething-design.md` §3.3 already authors a statement for this exact situation:**

> **A legal-but-pointless leg is NOT a Blocked state and does not take Stamp.** Where a second leg
> adds no risk — **one pick already contains the other** — the leg is legal, correctly priced, and
> added; the machine simply **states the fact in toner, in its own space**: *this adds nothing; the
> first already contains it.* **Silence there would let him be quietly charged for a leg that cannot
> lose, which is a cost he cannot see at the point of spending (S17).**

**RULED: these are ONE statement, not two.** Both are toner, both fire on *one pick contains the
other*, both are once per slip. **Two code paths would otherwise have shipped two toner sentences for
one fact** — which is T69/T70's defect in a third place, and §8's *never two unrelated clauses* in the
same slot.

**The §3.3 content governs, and the draft is a regression, because the draft drops the COST.** *This
adds nothing* is the whole reason the statement exists — S17, quoted above, is about him being
**quietly charged for a leg that cannot lose.** **`ONE OF THESE ALREADY COVERS THE OTHER` states the
structure and omits the consequence**, and the consequence is the only part he can act on.

**RE-AUTHOR against three requirements:**

1. **It states that the leg adds nothing** — the cost, not only the entailment.
2. **It says WHICH leg** — he may choose to remove it, and *one of these* gives him nothing to act on.
   §3.3's *the first already contains it* does this; the draft's symmetry loses it.
3. **One sentence, toner, once per slip** — this replaces both the §3.3 line and Draft 1; **there is
   not an implication warning AND a relation statement.**

**Shape offered, not imposed** — `THIS ADDS NOTHING; THE OTHER ALREADY COVERS IT.` — **su authors the
final form against the measured slot** and reports it. **The draft's instinct to withhold which leg
was right everywhere else in this batch and is wrong here**, because here the naming is the actionable
part, not decoration.

## S79 — THE 46.1% SILENCE. RULED CORRECT, and recorded so it is not rediscovered.

**Nearly half of placeable same-match slips state nothing, and that is canon working rather than a gap
to fill.** su asked for this to be ruled rather than left to be re-found. It is:

**S73 binds in the other direction from how a reviewer will first read it:** *where a correlation
cannot be labelled, the price does not move.* **A null `Principal` therefore means the price did NOT
move — so there is no cost to disclose, because nothing was taken.** **The statement exists to explain
a price that shortened; where nothing shortened, nothing is owed.** The silence is not an absence of
copy. **It is the absence of a thing to explain.**

**And the number is evidence of honesty, not of a gap.** If every slip carried a statement, the model
would be labelling relations it cannot name — **the precise failure S73 forbids.** **A high blank rate
is what a correctly-behaving model looks like from the surface.**

**RULED: 46.1% blank is CORRECT. A statement is never authored to fill it, and no future review
reopens it on the strength of the number alone.** `MutuallyExclusive` and `Independent` correctly
never appear as principals — the first is a refusal, the second is nothing to state.

## S77-am — THE FORMS. Approved with one re-authoring, and 13px ratified with its reason.

### The forms comply with S77 and the compliance is exact

**Causes are arity-keyed and name no leg** (`THESE TWO CANNOT BOTH WIN.` / `THESE THREE CANNOT ALL
WIN.`), **remedies name the act and the marks** (`RUB OUT BOTH MARKS TO PLACE.`) — **which is S77 as
ruled: the stamp states the act and its arity, the legs are marked in the flow and never named.**

**`WIN` governs, and batch 46's `land` is superseded in player-facing copy.** Both were in play;
**one word per thing**, and `WIN` is the plainer of the two for a sentence addressed to the player.
`land` remains fine in studio prose.

**`THIS PAYS LESS THAN IT COSTS.` is the best line in the set** — it states the cost in his terms with
no percentage, no coefficient and no comparison to a number he was never shown. It is exactly what
§8's *no formulae on the face* asks for and it was reached by re-authoring under S77's step (1)
rather than by shrinking anything.

### `NO RUB OUT FIXES THIS SLIP.` — NOT APPROVED. A remedy names an ACT.

**It is a cause-shaped string sitting in the remedy slot.** S73-am4 requires **cause AND remedy**, and
a remedy is *what to do* — **this tells him only that the thing he was about to try will not work, and
leaves him with no act at all.** **A refusal that closes every door is the one case where he most
needs to be told which door is open.**

**RULED: where no removal fixes the slip, the remedy names the act that does** — clearing it and
starting again — **in the word the actual control uses.** su binds it to the control that exists (this
seat is not authoring a control name blind) and **measures it with the rest.**

### 13px is ratified, and the reason is HEADROOM

**This seat's S77 row quoted `296 × 44` at 17px, from the PLACE control's own label size at
`SportsbookApp.cs:1103`. The stamp renders at 13px, and the measurement settles it in the build's
favour.** At 17px the widest form is **295.1px in a 296px control — nine tenths of a pixel** — and the
second widest is 288.2px. At 13px the widest is **225.7px, 76%**.

**su's reading is adopted verbatim as the rule:** *13px is not merely the ≥13px floor being respected;
it is the only one of the two with headroom, and headroom is what stops the next authored form from
reopening this.*

**RULED, and this is the durable half: every future stamp form is measured at 13px against 296px and
stays under 80%.** **C46 is why there is a budget at all** — a fixed box carries an unstated
assumption about the face it was sized against, and ~20% is what absorbs a face that measures wider.
**A form over 80% is re-authored, never accommodated.**
