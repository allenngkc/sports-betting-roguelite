B. Ticket Rail

The bet slip is a permanent left column: every leg stacked, the active one expanded, risk and payout at its foot. The match takes the remaining two-thirds. Cash-out anchors the base of the ticket column, so the money and the action live together.

Bet: the most literal expression of "the bet, not the sport, is the star" — you read your ticket first and the match second. Multi-leg tickets and ticket-to-ticket transitions are far clearer here than anywhere else, and the stats panel gets an obvious home as a second tab on the column. Risk: the stage loses centre stage, and a left-heavy composition fights the picked-team-attacks-right convention.

C. Full-Bleed Broadcast

The stage fills the entire screen. Everything else is an overlay floating on top of it, the way a real broadcast works — corner bug, lower-third, action card. Nothing is a panel.

Risk: overlays covering the pitch is exactly what PRD §8.6 forbids, and a retracting lower-third fights the requirement that zones never move. This is the highest-ceiling and highest-risk option.

D. Split Horizon

A hard horizontal division. The top half is the match and nothing else. The bottom half is a dense console holding everything about your money. Two worlds, one screen, no overlap.

Bet: the cleanest answer to the stats panel you just asked for — it expands inside the console and never touches the pitch, so §4.4 pausing and §8.6 no-obscuring both stay intact for free. Also the easiest to keep truthful, since match facts and money facts never share a region. Risk: giving half the screen to a console makes the stage small, and a small stage undercuts the scene-variety work in §7 that Phase 2 is about to build.

Bet: a vertical pitch is taller than it is wide, which suits a 16:9 screen far better than a letterboxed horizontal pitch, and it frees two full-height flanks for information without shrinking the stage. Formation reads beautifully vertically, which the §8.8 panel now needs. Risk: this changes the framing your PRD §4.5 approved. Direction stays stable — up instead of right — but it is a real departure from Decision B and needs an explicit ruling. It is also the least broadcast-like of the five.

The six questions (PRD §1), which every layout must answer in order:
  1. Who is playing, and which team or market did I back?
  2. What is the score and match time?
  3. What still has to happen for this leg to win?
  4. How much is at risk, and what does the ticket pay?
  5. Can I cash out right now? If not, why not?
  6. What event just changed the price?
