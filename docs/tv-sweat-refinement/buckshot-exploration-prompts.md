# Buckshot-blend exploration — three directions

**Date:** 2026-07-26 · **Requested by:** Allen · **Status:** exploration, nothing decided

Allen observed that the room reads Buckshot Roulette — grimy, claustrophobic, institutional — and
asked whether that style could blend into the sweat UI. These three prompts test how far it can go.

**Keep constant across all three:** P5's approved grade, haze, lifted blacks, film grain, and panel
physicality. The room does not change. Only the screen does. Same fixed scenario, same fictional
teams, so the three are comparable to each other and to P5.

**What is being put back on the table by looking at these:** the register was locked to *expensive
and slick* on 2026-07-25, and CRT/dither/scanline treatments were made an anti-reference when
`design/08-art-direction.md` was deprecated. Direction A reopens both. B and C do not.

---

## A — Buckshot-faithful

Honest to the reference. Low-fidelity, dithered, near-monochrome, degraded.

```
The approved bunker room at night: peeling paint, exposed conduit, riveted bunk frame, patched couch, deep-set window on a neon city, battered desk with laptop and ashtray, dim fluorescent strip, olive and khaki, heavy atmospheric haze, film grain over the whole frame, lifted shadows, shot as one photograph.

The wall-mounted television shows a live soccer betting display rendered in a deliberately LOW-FIDELITY, degraded style: coarse ordered dithering standing in for every tone, a restricted near-monochrome palette of sickly amber and dirty green on deep black with almost no other colour, chunky low-resolution letterforms, harsh contrast with crushed blacks and blown highlights, visible signal noise and interference banding, as if displayed on failing industrial equipment.

Screen contents, one match: a narrow left column of four stacked bet legs, one bright, two mid, one fully dark; a small crude top-down pitch of coarse dithered blocks with a handful of marker dots; a scoreline in blocky letterforms reading NORTHGATE and CALDER CITY; and a horizontal band at the foot showing a cash-out figure, the brightest element on the screen.

Oppressive, cheap, institutional, hostile. The television is the dominant light source, throwing sickly amber into the haze. Photographic depth of field, 16:9.
```

**Watch for:** this is the closest to the deprecated `08` world. If it reads as retro-arcade or
vaporwave rather than as failing industrial hardware, it has landed in the rut rather than the
reference. Also check whether four legs, a scoreline and a pitch survive at this fidelity — Buckshot
gets to show almost nothing, and this design cannot.

## B — Institutional equipment (the synthesis)

Buckshot's materiality without its degradation. The screen is hardened industrial hardware bolted
into the wall, not a consumer television — but it is sharp, and it works.

```
The approved bunker room at night: peeling paint, exposed conduit, riveted bunk frame, patched couch, deep-set window on a neon city, battered desk with laptop and ashtray, dim fluorescent strip, olive and khaki, heavy atmospheric haze, film grain over the whole frame, lifted shadows, shot as one photograph.

Mounted on the wall is not a consumer television but a HARDENED INDUSTRIAL DISPLAY — a heavy steel-framed control-room monitor bolted flush into the wall with visible rivets and a thick painted metal bezel, scuffed and chipped, with a small stencilled equipment code on the frame and a single physical indicator lamp beside it. The glass is thick and slightly recessed.

The display itself is SHARP AND HIGH RESOLUTION, not degraded: crisp technical type, hairline rules, precise numerals, a restrained palette of cold white and dim cyan on near-black with amber as the single accent, plus muted blue and magenta only to distinguish the two teams. Everything reads as instrumentation — a readout on equipment, not an app.

Screen contents, one match: a narrow left column listing four stacked bet legs of one ticket — one amber and won, two bright white and live, one fully unlit and dead; small figures for risk and payout beneath; and a horizontal amber band at the foot showing a cash-out figure, the single brightest element. To the right, a compact scoreline reading NORTHGATE and CALDER CITY, and below it a clean abstract top-down pitch with dim markings and small blue and magenta player dots.

Brutalist, utilitarian, expensive in a military way rather than a consumer way. The display is the dominant light source. Photographic depth of field, 16:9.
```

**Watch for:** whether "industrial" reads as *cheap* or as *serious hardware*. The target is a
military or control-room instrument — costly, hard-wearing, unglamorous. If it reads as a broken old
monitor, it has drifted into A.

## C — P5 restrained

Not a new direction. P5's approved screen with Buckshot's discipline applied: colour pulled back to
near-monochrome with one accent, type hardened, density reduced.

```
The approved bunker room at night: peeling paint, exposed conduit, riveted bunk frame, patched couch, deep-set window on a neon city, battered desk with laptop and ashtray, dim fluorescent strip, olive and khaki, heavy atmospheric haze, film grain over the whole frame, lifted shadows, shot as one photograph.

The wall-mounted flat television shows a live soccer betting broadcast, sharp and high resolution, seen slightly off-axis with a faint reflection of the fluorescent on its glass.

The screen is SEVERELY RESTRAINED IN COLOUR: almost entirely cold white and dim grey on near-black, with amber as the ONLY strong accent, and muted blue and magenta used sparingly and only to tell the two teams apart. No other colour anywhere. Type is hard, technical and uppercase. Rules are hairline. There is generous empty black space and far fewer elements than a betting app would show.

Screen contents, one match only: a narrow left column of four stacked bet legs — one dim amber won, two white and live, one completely unlit and dead — with small risk and payout figures, and a horizontal amber band at the foot carrying a cash-out figure as the single brightest element on the surface. To the right a compact scoreline reading NORTHGATE and CALDER CITY, and a spare abstract top-down pitch with very dim markings and small blue and magenta dots.

Austere, quiet, expensive, confident. Deep blacks and a great deal of empty space. The television is the dominant light source. Photographic depth of field, 16:9.
```

**Watch for:** whether restraint reads as *confidence* or as *unfinished*. This is the lowest-risk
option and the one that keeps everything already approved.

# D — the resolution (run this one)

**Allen's ruling, 2026-07-26:** B's enclosure, C's room layout, and a display fidelity **between A and
C**. A was too degraded; C was too high-fidelity.

The framing that resolves the fidelity question: **the display is a decade old and works perfectly.**
Not failing, not new. Maintained institutional equipment.

```
A cramped bunker room at night, painterly semi-realistic concept art, shot as one photograph. Heavily peeling paint on walls, ceiling and floor, exposed conduit and pipes, riveted steel construction. TWO heavy riveted industrial bunk frames — one above a worn patched sofa, one above a battered metal desk. A deep-set window on the far wall between them, looking onto a neon high-tech city skyline at night. The desk carries an open laptop and an ashtray of cigarette butts. A metal stool, a coiled black cable. One dim sickly yellow-green fluorescent strip. Olive, khaki and drab green surfaces, damp and institutional. Heavy atmospheric haze, film grain across the whole frame, lifted shadows never pure black, gentle vignette, subtle chromatic aberration at the edges. Filmic and cohesive.

On its own wall is a HARDENED INDUSTRIAL DISPLAY, not a television: a heavy steel housing bolted flush into the wall with visible rivets around a thick chipped painted bezel, the glass recessed into the frame, a small stencilled equipment code on the metal, one physical indicator lamp beside it, and conduit feeding into the housing continuous with the room's pipe runs. It looks installed by an institution, not bought by the occupant, and built from the same riveted painted language as the bunk frames.

THE DISPLAY IS OLD BUT WORKING PERFECTLY — NOT FAILING AND NOT NEW. Its readout is sharp and legible but built on a visibly COARSE grid: crisp forms with slightly chunky edges, never sub-pixel smooth, never dithered or noisy. Solid drawn 1-2 pixel rules, no delicate hairlines. Technical uppercase type of medium weight, slightly condensed. A restricted palette with no gradients and no subtle greys: warm amber as the dominant colour and the only accent, cold white for facts, and muted blue and magenta used sparingly only to distinguish two teams, on near-black. A slight unevenness in the backlight and a soft amber bloom, but absolutely no scanlines, no screen curvature, no interference or signal noise.

Screen contents, one match only: a narrow left column listing four bet legs of a single ticket — one amber and won, two white and live, one fully unlit and dead — with small risk and payout figures below, and at the foot a solid amber band showing a cash-out figure in dollars, the single brightest element anywhere on the screen. To the right, a compact scoreline reading the invented teams NORTHGATE and CALDER CITY with large white numerals and a match clock, and beneath it a spare abstract top-down pitch with dim markings and small blue and magenta player dots.

The display is the dominant light source, throwing amber through the haze onto the peeling walls, the bunks and the floor. Deep shadow elsewhere. Photographic depth of field, 16:9.
```

## What to judge

1. **Is the fidelity right?** Sharp but coarse — not dithered like A, not delicate like C. This is the
   only genuinely open question left.
2. **Does the enclosure read as installed rather than bought?** Riveted, painted, maintained.
3. **Amber dominant** — the room lead is waiting on this to retune the light rig, so confirm it works
   before they do.
4. Cash-out band is the brightest thing; dead leg still reads dead.

If this lands, the visual direction is closed and everything remaining is implementation.

# F — monochrome amber, D's fidelity (supersedes E)

**Allen, 2026-07-27:** the palette is **concept A's amber**, confirmed against the A render. E's cool
blue/magenta restoration was our misreading of "the previous colours" and is void.

F is the assembly of everything approved: **A's palette · D's fidelity · B's enclosure · C's two-bunk
room.** Two new design answers appear here for the first time, both forced by going monochrome — see
the judge list.

```
A cramped bunker room at night, painterly semi-realistic concept art, shot as one photograph. Heavily peeling paint on walls, ceiling and floor, exposed conduit and pipes, riveted steel construction. TWO heavy riveted industrial bunk frames — one above a worn patched sofa, one above a battered metal desk. A deep-set window on the far wall between them looking onto a neon high-tech city skyline at night. The desk carries an open laptop and an ashtray of cigarette butts. A metal stool, a coiled black cable. One dim sickly yellow-green fluorescent strip. Olive, khaki and drab green surfaces, damp and institutional. Heavy atmospheric haze, film grain across the whole frame, lifted shadows never pure black, gentle vignette, subtle chromatic aberration. Filmic and cohesive.

On its own wall is a HARDENED INDUSTRIAL DISPLAY, not a television: heavy steel housing bolted flush into the wall with visible rivets around a thick chipped painted bezel, glass recessed into the frame, a small stencilled equipment code on the metal, one physical indicator lamp beside it, conduit feeding into the housing continuous with the room's pipe runs. Installed by an institution, not bought by the occupant.

THE SCREEN IS MONOCHROME AMBER ON NEAR-BLACK. One single warm amber hue and nothing else — no blue, no magenta, no white, no other colour anywhere on the display. Everything is distinguished by BRIGHTNESS ALONE: bright amber for current facts, mid amber for context and labels, very dim amber for structure and pending items, and completely unlit black for anything dead.

THE DISPLAY IS OLD BUT WORKING PERFECTLY — NOT FAILING AND NOT NEW. Sharp and legible on a visibly COARSE grid: crisp forms with slightly chunky edges, never sub-pixel smooth. Solid drawn 1-2 pixel rules, no delicate hairlines. Technical uppercase type, medium weight, slightly condensed. Slight unevenness in the backlight and a soft amber bloom, but absolutely NO scanlines, NO screen curvature, NO interference or signal noise, NO dithering.

Screen contents, one match only. A narrow left column listing four bet legs of a single ticket: one bright and won, two bright and live, one completely unlit and black. Small risk and payout figures below. At the foot, a CASH OUT band rendered INVERTED — a solid filled block of bright amber with the words and the dollar figure punched out of it in dark — the only inverted element anywhere on the screen and the brightest thing on the display. To the right, a compact scoreline reading the invented teams NORTHGATE and CALDER CITY with large amber numerals and a match clock. Below it a spare abstract top-down pitch drawn in dim amber lines, on which the two teams are told apart NOT by colour but by SHAPE: one team's players are SOLID FILLED amber dots, the other team's are HOLLOW AMBER RINGS.

The display is the dominant light source, throwing warm amber through the haze onto the peeling walls, the bunks and the floor. Deep shadow elsewhere. Photographic depth of field, 16:9.
```

## What to judge

Two of these are new answers to problems monochrome creates, and they are the point of this render:

1. **Can you tell the two teams apart on the pitch?** Filled dots versus hollow rings, since hue is
   gone. This is the riskiest consequence of monochrome and PRD §1 question 1 depends on it. If it
   fails at a glance, we need a different form language, not a colour.
2. **Does the inverted cash-out band read as the action?** With everything amber, gold can no longer
   mean money, so inversion carries it instead — a solid block with dark type punched out. It should
   be unmistakably the one thing you can act on.
3. Fidelity unchanged from D, palette matching A.
4. Dead leg still reads dead.

If those two new answers land, the visual direction is closed.

## What to judge

1. **Is the screen cold and colourless with one gold bar** — C's palette — rather than amber or
   vivid? This is the whole point.
2. **Is the fidelity coarser than C** but not degraded like A or F?
3. **Is more blue coming from the window**, with the fluorescent still slightly yellow?
4. **Does the dark bunk above the desk read as occupied** without being legible as empty?
5. At a glance, can you separate the two teams' dots? If not, the fix is **form** — filled versus
   hollow — not more saturation. Adding saturation is what produced E.

## How to judge the earlier three

Compare all three against P5, not against each other first.

1. **Does it still belong in the room?** The question that killed two earlier directions.
2. **Can you answer the six questions?** A is most at risk here.
3. **Does it read as equipment or as software?** All three should say equipment.
4. **Is it still expensive?** If a direction reads cheap, that is a register change, and the register
   was locked deliberately — worth making that choice knowingly rather than by drift.
5. **Rut check.** Does A look like failing industrial hardware, or like retro-arcade nostalgia? The
   second is the world already deprecated.

If B or C wins, `DESIGN.md` needs a palette revision and little else. If A wins, the register and the
CRT anti-reference both need formally reopening, and `DESIGN.md` §2 gets rewritten a second time.
